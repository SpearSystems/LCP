Decision (maintainer, 2026-11-04)

Accepted as a MINOR, additive, capability-gated surface, on the terms of D8:

# Contract element Normative rule (this record)
C1 New types quote and counter join the closed message.type enum (SPEC.md §6). Absence of a type is unchanged; unknown types remain LCP-006.
C2 Capability gating A sender MUST NOT emit either type, nor bid.negotiation_binding_token, nor ack.negotiation, unless the receiver's capabilities().features advertises negotiation and its parsed-semver lcp_versions opens the v1.2 gate (Compatibility, below).
C3 Off by default LCP_FEATURE_NEGOTIATION defaults to off in the reference platform: no advertisement, no intake, no state written. CI exercises the surface with the flag on.
C4 Ping-safe Integer minor units, ISO 4217 currency, ids, timestamps, round counters, closed reason values. No consumer PII, no contact hash, no free text. Strict allowlist (additionalProperties: false).
C5 Bounded rounds max_rounds default 3, hard cap 5; rounds_remaining counts buyer counters; a counter at zero is refused with round_limit_exceeded.
C6 Monotone Every counter.price_cents MUST be ≥ the sender's own most recent price in that negotiation (strictly greater for the buyer), so no message ever moves the price against the platform. Never below the floor; below floor is refused, never clamped.
C7 Binding token An accept produces a single-use binding_token that replaces bid_price_cents on the buyer's subsequent bid. Replay of the same redemption returns the original outcome; no second bid, no second payable.
C8 Absolute expiry expires_at is absolute, restated on every message, and MUST NOT exceed the ping's TTL. Past expiry the negotiation is EXPIRED; late messages are refused negotiation_expired.

Conditions of acceptance (the "Traceability and implementation gate" of governance/LEP.md):

  2. schemas/ stays the only normative source: both payload schemas, the envelope.json enum addition, the bid.json token field, and the ack.json negotiation object are edited there first, then generated or verified downstream (10 SDKs, OpenAPI, MCP, vectors).
  3. Any deviation from the wire shape in this record returns it to Review before merge.
  4. The registry row keeps the 2027-05-04 re-evaluation date until adopter evidence replaces the waiver.

Problem statement and evidence

LCP has exactly one price-exchange round. A ping advertises a lead to prospective buyers with floor_price_cents and expires_at (schemas/ping.json), a buyer answers once with bid{decision, bid_price_cents} (schemas/bid.json), and the platform selects the winner ("The platform collects bids within the ping expiry window"; SPEC.md §4). There is no second round, no seller counter, and no way for a machine buyer to converge on a price it could not state in one shot.

That is a structural gap for automated buyers, and it shows up in three concrete ways today:

  1. A buyer cannot improve a losing bid. A bid that loses on price produces no further message ("losers receive no further messages" — the same paragraph states the property as a privacy feature). A pricing agent that would have closed at an intermediate price has no protocol turn in which to say so.
  2. The only price signal is binary. decision is accept | reject | pass, and reject_reason is prose-shaped and open. A platform cannot distinguish "no interest" from "close on price" without reading a free string, which is exactly the kind of field a PII audit removes rather than extends.
  3. Buyer-side automation has no auditable price history. Every negotiated price that happens off-protocol (email, chat, a sales rep's call) leaves no trail LCP can reconcile against post.pricing. Disputes then compare a delivered price with an e-mail thread.

Evidence status — stated plainly. This is built under the D1 trigger waiver. The v1.1 trigger for this item (docs/V1.1-ROADMAP.md, deferred-trigger table: "Agent-to-agent price negotiation | Agent bidding volume large enough that static floor pricing is suboptimal") was not met: no adopter reported agent bidding volume at that scale, no interoperability test demanded a counter message, and no second independent adopter asked for the surface. governance/LEP.md decision rules require, before a new universal message type, "evidence from at least two independent adopters or a documented interoperability necessity, plus an extension feasibility analysis"; neither the two-adopter evidence nor a documented necessity exists, and the extension feasibility analysis (Alternatives, A and C) does not produce one. The maintainer waived the trigger: D1 says the waiving record supersedes a Deferral "by maintainer decision, not by new adopter evidence".

What this record does instead of inventing adoption: it names the deterministic evidence that stands in for it — the executable vector suite and the platform tests enumerated under "Tests", which fail loudly if rounds, monotonicity, floor refusal, expiry, or token idempotency regress — plus the dated re-evaluation at 2027-05-04, where a reader may challenge the decision with actual usage data. The honest summary is: the mechanism is built because the maintainer chose to fund it, not because the need was proven.

Scope

  • Core protocol (additive MINOR): two values added to the closed message.type enum (quote, counter); two new strict-allowlist payload schemas with integer-cent pricing and no PII; one optional bid field (negotiation_binding_token); one optional ack object (negotiation); one advertisement key in capabilities().features (negotiation). No envelope shape change, no new required field, no vertical-schema change, no error-code addition (refusals ride the existing open rejection_reason).
  • Reference platform: negotiation state machine with audit, round and floor enforcement at one decision point, single-use token storage (hash only), expiry sweep, tenant authorization, metrics, and the LCP_FEATURE_NEGOTIATION switch — W4-08 and W5-01…W5-03.
  • SDKs, OpenAPI, MCP, conformance: typed models and helpers in all ten SDKs, POST /quotes + POST /counters + redemption on POST /bids, send_quote / send_counter MCP tools, a new vector suite, coverage-matrix rows — W5-04 and the impact section below.
  • Not in scope: pricing strategy (no normative guidance on how a party should choose a price — that is agent policy, deliberately not protocol); seller-side concessions (C6 forbids a platform counter below its standing ask; a cheaper price is a new negotiation, not a counter); ceiling semantics beyond the ping's floor (offer-side max_allowed_price_cents already exists for post-time guardrails and is unchanged); negotiation over non-ping surfaces (post reprice, resale, subscriptions); multi-currency conversion (D7 applies unchanged — a negotiation is single-currency and refuses a mismatch rather than converting); any change to post.pricing, payability, disputes, or refunds; a read/list HTTP surface (Deferred question Q6).

Proposed wire shape

1. Envelope

Element Requirement
message.type "quote" or "counter". The enum stays closed; an unknown value is still LCP-006 UNKNOWN_MESSAGE_TYPE (SPEC.md §6: "Adding a message type is a MINOR version bump").
lcp.version 1.2.0 or a later minor. A receiver advertising 1.0.x/1.1.x only is never a legal recipient (C2).
message.correlation_id quote: the message.id of the ping being priced (required, non-null). counter: the message.id of the quote/counter it answers (required, non-null). The chain is the negotiation's audit spine.
message.idempotency_key Required, exactly as for every other type. Same key + same bytes → the original ack; same key + different bytes → LCP-005 (SPEC.md §4 idempotency rule).
message.sender_id / receiver_id The platform and the pinged buyer, in one of the two directions. A third party is LCP-002 UNKNOWN_SENDER (authorization axis).
message.security HMAC-SHA256 per SPEC.md §9, or the HTTP binding's X-LCP-Timestamp/X-LCP-Signature. Negotiation messages are never unsigned.
message.test Honored; test negotiation traffic follows LCP-013 and never mints a billable bid.
payload Strict allowlist (additionalProperties: false) on both types. No free-text field of any kind is defined (no note, no message, no buyer_reference), because a free string is the PII-smuggling shape this surface must not carry.

Both payloads are ping-class: they inherit the prohibition a ping carries (§4 "PII: None"), so the ping PII code applies to them, and the D9 taxonomy classifies every property internal (commercial terms) — never contact, restricted, or prohibited.

2. quote — the platform opens a negotiation

Sent by the platform (seller) to a buyer it has already pinged. It states the ask for a named ping and opens the bounded round structure.

Field Type Req Rule
negotiation_id string yes Opaque platform-assigned id, ^neg_[A-Za-z0-9_-]{8,64}$, unique per negotiation. The only lookup key a later message may use.
ping_id string yes The ping this negotiation prices. The ping supplies the floor, the currency, and the TTL ceiling. A ping_id the receiver never sent that buyer is LCP-002.
offer_id string yes The buyer offer being priced; MUST equal the ping's offer_id (LCP-100 on mismatch).
price_cents integer minimum: 0 yes The ask in minor units of currency. MUST be ≥ floor_price_cents; below floor is refused with rejection_reason: "below_floor_price" and never clamped.
currency ISO 4217 alpha-3 yes MUST equal the ping's currency (D7: no cross-currency comparison, no FX in a negotiation). Mismatch → LCP-100.
floor_price_cents integer minimum: 0 yes Restated from the ping so the buyer can verify the floor and the monotone rule without re-reading the ping. MUST equal the ping's value; mismatch → LCP-100 (field: "payload.floor_price_cents").
max_rounds integer 1..5, default 3 no Rounds this negotiation may run. Absent = 3 (D8 default). > 5 → LCP-100 (hard cap; not a deployment knob).
rounds_remaining integer 0..5 yes Buyer counters still available at the moment this message is sent. On the opening quote this equals the effective max_rounds (a quote consumes no round).
expires_at RFC 3339 UTC yes Absolute end of the negotiation. MUST NOT be later than the ping's expires_at, nor later than the ping's timestamp plus its TTL when only a TTL was expressed; either failure is LCP-100 and no negotiation is created.
rejection_reason string (open enum) cond. Present only on a terminal refusal message (no negotiation opened): below_floor_price, round_limit_exceeded, negotiation_expired, unknown_negotiation, negotiation_closed. When present, price_cents MUST be absent and the ack for the triggering message carries the same reason.

3. counter — either party answers the standing message

Sent by the buyer or the platform, strictly alternating (no two consecutive counter messages from one sender in one negotiation; a violation is LCP-004 INVALID_STATUS_TRANSITION at the receiver, not a new code).

Field Type Req Rule
negotiation_id string yes MUST name an existing negotiation for this (tenant, buyer, ping) triple; otherwise unknown_negotiation (or LCP-002 when the negotiation belongs to another buyer/tenant).
ping_id string yes MUST equal the negotiation's ping; mismatch → LCP-100.
offer_id string yes MUST equal the negotiation's offer; mismatch → LCP-100.
decision string, closed enum yes counter (move the price), accept (close at the price of the message being answered), refuse (close with a reason).
price_cents integer minimum: 0 cond. Required unless decision = refuse. counter: subject to the monotone rule below and ≥ floor_price_cents. accept: MUST equal the price_cents of the message it answers; any other value is LCP-100.
currency ISO 4217 alpha-3 yes MUST equal the negotiation currency; mismatch → LCP-100. Carried on every message so a refusal is self-describing without a lookup.
floor_price_cents integer minimum: 0 yes Restated and checked against the negotiation's floor; mismatch → LCP-100.
rounds_remaining integer 0..5 yes The sender's view of the buyer counters still available at the moment this message is sent, i.e. before it is applied. A stale value (not equal to the platform's stored value) is LCP-100 — it is the cheap ordering/replay guard that makes out-of-order delivery fail instead of corrupting the count.
expires_at RFC 3339 UTC yes The negotiation's absolute end, unchanged. MUST NOT exceed the value set by the opening quote; later → LCP-100. A message whose message.timestamp is at or after expires_at is refused negotiation_expired.
rejection_reason string (open enum) cond. Required when decision = refuse; absent otherwise. Known values (open enumeration on the ack side already exists, schemas/ack.json): below_floor_price, non_monotone_counter, round_limit_exceeded, negotiation_expired, unknown_negotiation, negotiation_closed.

Monotone rule (normative, C6). For one negotiation, let p_sender be the sender's own most recent price_cents in that negotiation, or null when the sender has not stated a price yet (the platform's opening quote is its p_sender from then on):

  • p_sender is null — the counter is bounded by the floor alone: price_cents ≥ floor_price_cents, so a buyer's opening counter may sit exactly on the floor (the floor rule below is what refuses anything lower);
  • a buyer counter with p_sender present MUST satisfy price_cents > p_sender and price_cents ≥ floor_price_cents;
  • a platform counter with p_sender present MUST satisfy price_cents ≥ p_sender (it may restate the ask, never lower it) and price_cents ≥ floor_price_cents;
  • accept is not a counter and is not constrained by the monotone rule — a platform MAY accept any buyer price at or above the floor, which is how a concession is expressed (a concession is an accept, never a lower counter).

Two consequences, stated because they are the design and not an accident:

  1. The price path of a negotiation never decreases. The buyer climbs; the platform restates. A buyer counter at or above the standing ask terminates the negotiation accepted at the counter price (the platform receives at least its ask — never silently downgrade the winner's price).
  2. A platform that wants to sell cheaper must open a new negotiation. Seller-side price discovery downward is a pricing decision with its own audit trail; smuggling it in as a lower counter is what C6 forbids.

4. Round accounting and termination

  • max_rounds is per negotiation; the effective value is the opening quote's max_rounds or 3 when absent, and never exceeds 5.
  • Only a buyer message with decision: "counter" consumes a round: rounds_remaining decrements by one when the platform accepts the counter into state. accept and refuse are terminal and consume nothing, and a refused message never moves the price or burns a round — the only mutation a refusal causes is the terminal REFUSED state itself.
  • A buyer counter received when rounds_remaining is 0 is refused round_limit_exceeded; the negotiation moves to REFUSED and is never reopened. A new price discussion is a new negotiation_id.
  • accept from either side is terminal-accepted: the platform records ACCEPTED at the accepted price and mints the binding token below.
  • refuse, a floor violation, a monotone violation, an expiry, or round exhaustion all terminate REFUSED (or EXPIRED) with exactly one rejection_reason. There is no "retry the same negotiation" state: a refused negotiation id is permanently closed, and a later message naming it is refused negotiation_closed.

5. Binding token (C7)

An accept is agreement; it is not yet a bid. The platform answers the accepting message with an ack carrying:

{
  "negotiation": {
    "negotiation_id": "neg_7c1f0a9b2e4d",
    "outcome": "accepted",
    "price_cents": 10000,
    "currency": "USD",
    "rounds_remaining": 2,
    "binding_token": "tok_9fQ2x8Lm4Vb7Kd1Rt6Yp3Wc5Nz0Hs2Ja",
    "binding_expires_at": "2026-11-04T12:10:00Z"
  }
}
Field Type Rule
negotiation_id string The negotiation this outcome belongs to.
outcome string, closed enum opened, countered, accepted, refused, expired, redeemed. Present on every ack for a quote/counter (and on the bid ack of a successful redemption), so a buyer never has to infer state from prose.
price_cents integer The accepted, countered, or refused price (absent on refused when the refusal carried no price).
currency ISO 4217 The negotiation currency.
rounds_remaining integer The post-message value.
binding_token string, ^tok_[A-Za-z0-9_-]{32,128}$ Present only when outcome = "accepted". Single-use. Returned exactly once (the ack to the accepting message); the platform stores only a hash, mirroring api_key_hash, and never logs or echoes the token again.
binding_expires_at RFC 3339 UTC ≤ the negotiation expires_at. A token is unusable at or after this instant.

Redemption. The buyer presents the token in a bid (new optional field, schemas/bid.json): negotiation_binding_token, with ping_id equal to the negotiation's ping, decision: "accept", and bid_price_cents either absent or exactly the token's price. Rules:

  1. First redemption creates the bid at the token price and moves the negotiation to REDEEMED. The bid is then an ordinary bid for every downstream purpose (auction, post, payability) — the token is a price carrier, never a money shortcut. The acknowledgment for the redemption is the bid's normal ack with negotiation.outcome: "redeemed", the redeemed price, and no binding_token.
  2. A replay of the same redemption (same idempotency_key, same bytes) returns the original ack byte for byte, creates no second bid, and cannot produce a second payable.
  3. A token presented after REDEEMED, or after binding_expires_at, is refused negotiation_closed — except the same-sender same-key replay in (2), which is "replay returns the original outcome", not a reuse.
  4. A token presented by any other sender or tenant is LCP-002.
  5. Token format or checksum failure is LCP-100; an unknown hash is negotiation_closed (never "invalid token" — that would confirm token existence).

      "decision": "counter",
      "price_cents": 7000,
      "currency": "USD",
      "floor_price_cents": 6000,
      "rounds_remaining": 1,
      "expires_at": "2026-11-04T12:10:00Z"
    }
  }
}

Rejected: the buyer's previous price in this negotiation was 8000, so 7000 walks the price backwards. Refused non_monotone_counter; no round is consumed and no state changes on a rejected message, but the negotiation is terminal REFUSED (C6/C8: the id does not become reusable). The platform's mirror case — a counter below its own standing ask — is refused the same way; it is a concession dressed as a counter.

Rejected twice over: consumer_phone and note are outside the strict allowlist, so the message fails LCP-100 naming payload.consumer_phone; because the field name is a consumer identifier (and a phone number is a contact value, not a price term), the ping-class prohibition applies and LCP-008 PII_IN_PING is returned with it. The code is reused rather than renamed — a new code would be a receiver-visible change a v1.0 peer cannot anticipate (SPEC.md §5). Both the value and the reason are recorded as a security finding, never stored as a payload.

Privacy, security, and universal-core audit

Ping-safe proof (field by field). Every field of every message in this surface is one of: an opaque platform id (negotiation_id, ping_id, offer_id, sender_id, receiver_id, correlation_id, original_message_id, binding_token), an integer in minor units (price_cents, floor_price_cents, rounds_remaining, max_rounds), an ISO 4217 code, a UTC timestamp, or a value from a closed/known reason enumeration. None of them is derived from a consumer, and — unlike the contacted-hash surface — no consumer identifier is hashed into this surface at all: negotiation happens before delivery, so there is nothing to dedup and a hash would only create a per-pair oracle nobody needs. The allowlist (additionalProperties: false) is the enforcement, not prose: unknown fields fail validation, and a consumer-shaped name fails the ping-class PII rule (invalid example E). The conformance runner gains the same three checks it applies to pings (allowlist, no PII block, no contact digest) for both new types, and the D9 x-lcp-pii annotation is required on every property — all internal.

No free text (stated because it is the design constraint, not an oversight). The surface defines no note, comment, description, reason_text, or buyer_reference. Refusal reasons are enumeration values, so an operator cannot explain a refusal in the field where a consumer name would otherwise be typed. Anything a party wants to say in prose belongs in its own systems (LEP-0007 takes the same position for details).

Authorization and tenancy. A negotiation is scoped to one (tenant, buyer_id, ping_id, offer_id) tuple. The buyer must be one the platform pinged for that offer; the platform must be the receiver_id of the ping the buyer bids against. A negotiation_id presented by any other principal is LCP-002 — the id is not a bearer credential, and the response is indistinguishable from "no such negotiation" so it cannot be used to probe existence. Token redemption is bound to (negotiation_id, buyer_id, price, expiry); a foreign or expired token is negotiation_closed, never "invalid". Every route carries the tenant predicate (tenant_id) and the existing test_tenant_isolation gate (execution-plan invariant I6).

Replay and duplication. Envelope HMAC plus the five-minute replay window (SPEC §9, LCP-012) apply unchanged. Idempotency keys are mandatory; same key/different bytes is LCP-005. A duplicate counter returns the original ack and does not consume a second round. A duplicate redemption returns the original ack and creates no second bid. Tokens are unguessable, single-use, stored as hashes, and never logged (the existing operational rule: "audit rows, metrics, and logs never carry token bytes").

Money and price integrity. All arithmetic is on integer minor units (D7 unchanged; no FX, no float, no rounding in this surface). A mismatch between the restated floor_price_cents/currency and the platform's stored values is LCP-100, never a silent correction. Below-floor prices are refused, never clamped, so the platform never claims a price the buyer did not state. An accepted price is the bid price: the token carries it, redemption cannot restate it, and the ordinary auction/post payability path is the only thing that can create money. Negotiation messages never create a payable, never touch post.pricing, and never re-open a payable (D2's rule generalizes).

Abuse surfaces, bounded. Negotiation-id enumeration is infeasible (opaque random ids, authenticated pair scope, non-oracular error). Price probing is bounded by max_rounds (≤ 5) and by the existing per-sender rate limits (LCP-011). Floor mining discloses nothing new: the floor is already in the ping. Round-loop DoS is capped by C5. Forgotten negotiations are swept by expiry, so an unbounded id population cannot accumulate.

Retention and erasure. Negotiation rows contain no consumer data, so they are non-PII routing/audit records and are retained under the platform's non-PII class (W4-06), not the envelope class. privacy erase-lead redacts persisted envelopes and cancels pending deliveries; because a negotiation holds only ids, prices, and timestamps, erasure has nothing to redact here and MUST NOT delete the negotiation's audit trail — but it MUST also not be usable as a re-identification path, and the blocking test asserts there is no such field to find. Tokens are hashed; erasure leaves a hash that grants nothing after REDEEMED/expiry.

Universal-core audit (SPEC §12 items). No market vocabulary (no vertical, country, channel, or product term appears). No executable matching rule, no expression language, no callback. No hidden PII flow (the whole point of C4). No new dependency (stdlib only). No change to any existing required field, no change to a v1.0/v1.1 payload, and no change to an existing error code. One stable enunciation per semantic (a price is price_cents + currency, exactly as in bid/post/ping — no parallel spelling).

Compatibility, versioning, and deprecation impact

  • Closed-enum addition → MINOR. SPEC.md §6 states message.type is closed and "adding a message type is a MINOR version bump". v1.2.0 is that bump. The enum stays closed, so a receiver that has not implemented the types rejects them LCP-006 loudly rather than passing them through.
  • A 1.0/1.1 peer must never receive these types unsolicited. Emission is gated on two independent facts: (a) the receiver's parsed-semver lcp_versions contains major == 1 and minor >= 2 (SPEC §8 membership rule — parsed elements, never string prefixes), and (b) the receiver's capabilities().features map contains negotiation (W0-03 spine, {"batch": "1.2.0", "negotiation": "1.2.0", …}). Both are required because a deployment may run the code with the feature off. Capability absence means the sender must not emit; emitting anyway is a conformance failure, and the receiver answers LCP-006 (documented, not a crash).
  • ack.negotiation and bid.negotiation_binding_token are additive optionals on closed objects, gated by the same advertisement. A v1.1 receiver never sees them; a v1.2 receiver tolerates absence and treats their absence as "no negotiation" (the pre-existing behaviour).
  • The other direction is untouched. A 1.2 receiver accepting a 1.0 or 1.1 sender behaves exactly as it did: no new required field, no new rejection on an old message, no changed default. Adding a minor to lcp_versions never invalidates a lower-minor sender (SPEC §2/§8 rule).
  • Deprecation. Nothing is deprecated by this record. If the surface is superseded later, the N+2 compatibility window and two-year grace period of governance/LEP.md step 7 apply to two message types that no non-advertising peer can receive — the smallest possible deprecation surface.
  • Contingency. If implementation does not complete, docs/DECISIONS-v1.2.md moves the wire surface to v1.3.0 while the LEP record stays Accepted with a Design-Complete/not-implemented status in the registry. The registry status must never claim Implemented without the vector and platform evidence links.

SDK, OpenAPI, MCP, conformance, and operational impact

Schemas (normative source, edited first): schemas/envelope.json (message.type +2 values), new schemas/quote.json and schemas/counter.json (strict allowlist, x-lcp-pii: internal on every property, max_rounds/rounds_remaining bounds, conditional price_cents), schemas/bid.json (+negotiation_binding_token), schemas/ack.json (+negotiation object, closed outcome enum). $defs.currency is reused from core.json; no new core definition.

Ten SDKs. Each ships: Quote/Counter payload models (closed-enum discriminated message unions where the language has them), a negotiation helper (send_quote, send_counter, accept_negotiation, refuse_negotiation), token redemption on the existing bid call (submit_bid(..., negotiation_binding_token=…)), negotiation awareness in the capabilities model, and updated schema manifests. Per language — typescript, python, go, java, kotlin, rust, csharp, php, ruby, swift (implementations/sdk/<lang>) — the acceptance is: tools/check_schema_sync.py green for all ten, per-SDK compile/test green in sdk-interop.yml, and the closed-enum corpus (test-vectors/sdk/enum-passthrough-corpus.json) still refusing unknown message.type values while accepting quote/counter only when advertised.

OpenAPI (api/lcp-openapi.yaml): POST /quotes (operationId: submitQuote), POST /counters (submitCounter), redemption on the existing POST /bids (submitBid, with negotiation_binding_token on BidPayload), Capabilities.features as an additional-properties string map, Ack.negotiation and NegotiationOutcome schemas, and two tags (Negotiations). The change is additive: no operation, parameter, or response shape changes for a client that does not use it. The check-openapi gate asserts pattern equivalence between the OpenAPI enums and the JSON Schemas.

MCP (implementations/mcp-server/lcp_mcp_server/server.py, currently submit_lead, submit_call, query_lead_status, get_schema, get_capabilities, get_request_history, list_offers, submit_bid):

  • send_quote — platform-role tool; arguments mirror the payload table.
  • send_counter — buyer- or platform-role tool; decision explicit, so an agent cannot "counter" without stating a price.
  • redeem_negotiation_token — a thin wrapper over submit_bid with the token argument, present so an agent does not have to know the bid field name.
  • get_capabilities gains features pass-through verbatim (no filtering, so an agent sees the same advertisement a wire peer sees).

Conformance. New suite test-vectors/negotiation.json (ids NEG-001…), run by test-vectors/conformance.py, covering at minimum: valid quote/counter/accept round trip; below-floor refusal with no clamping; round exhaustion; expired message; non-monotone (buyer and platform directions); max_rounds > 5; expires_at beyond the ping TTL; currency/floor mismatch; duplicate counter (no round burned); token redemption and same-key replay; foreign-sender token; a quote/counter carrying a consumer field or a 48–64-hex contact digest; and an unadvertised receiver. The runner treats a receiver that does not advertise negotiation as skip, not fail (the advertisement is the switch), and the new coverage-matrix rows cite this LEP for every emission and refusal rule. Vector IDs are stable and never reused.

Platform state machine (W4-08/W5-03). One table per negotiation, one audit row per message, one hashed-token table:

State Entered by Terminal Notes
OPEN quote accepted at intake and persisted no The ask is recorded; rounds_remaining = max_rounds.
COUNTERED buyer decision: "counter" accepted into state no rounds_remaining decremented by one; new standing price recorded.
ACCEPTED decision: "accept" from either side, at an allowed price no (redeemable) Price frozen; one token hash minted; binding_expires_at recorded.
REDEEMED first valid token redemption in a bid yes The bid row exists; all downstream money semantics are the bid's.
REFUSED any terminal refusal (below_floor_price, non_monotone_counter, round_limit_exceeded, negotiation_expired, unknown_negotiation) yes Exactly one reason stored; the id never reopens. A later message naming a closed id is answered negotiation_closed with no state change.
EXPIRED expiry sweep past expires_at yes Sweep is idempotent; a late message is negotiation_expired, not a state change.

Illegal transitions return LCP-004 INVALID_STATUS_TRANSITION (REDEEMED → anything; ACCEPTED → COUNTERED; a second accept from the same negotiation). Audit event names follow the platform's dotted convention (negotiation.opened, negotiation.countered, negotiation.accepted, negotiation.refused, negotiation.expired, negotiation.redeemed) and record actor, before/after state, price, round, and request id — never token bytes.

Operations. GET /v1/lcp/metrics gains negotiation.{opened,countered,accepted,refused,expired,redeemed} counters, negotiation.rounds_used (histogram 0–5), negotiation.refusals_by_reason, and negotiation.token_redemption_failures. Operator guidance (docs update): a nonzero sustained token_redemption_failures is a client bug or a token leak; a round_limit_exceeded share above half means the cap is miscalibrated or the ask is unrealistic; refusals must never alert as errors (they are the designed refusal path). LCP_FEATURE_NEGOTIATION is documented beside LCP_MAX_DELIVERY_ATTEMPTS and the other LCP_* switches in the deployment guide; it is the only new switch for this surface (no per-tenant sub-flags, no per-peer toggles).

Alternatives considered

A. Reuse ping/bid with extension fields (rejected). The obvious cheapest route: add counter_price_cents, round, negotiation_id to bid and let the platform "counter" by sending another ping. It fails on four measured properties of the current core:

  1. ping and bid are both strict allowlists (schemas/ping.json and schemas/bid.json, additionalProperties: false), so a v1.1 receiver rejects the new fields LCP-100/LCP-008 — the same compatibility wall D8 exists to avoid, only spread across two existing types instead of two new ones.
  2. A ping is a broadcast to many buyers and carries lead_age_minutes, quality signals, and slot state. Re-pinging to counter would either re-broadcast the counter (leaking one buyer's price to its competitors — a commercial-information breach) or require a private ping variant, which is a new type in disguise.
  3. bid.decision is a three-value closed enum with no round semantics; adding counter to it makes decision: counter mean "a message that will be answered", which is a state machine living in a field that has none.
  4. bid has no floor echo and no round counter, so a receiver cannot verify the monotone rule or detect replay — the exact ambiguity this record removes.

The extension route was therefore rejected as more wire surface, not less: a new type with a strict allowlist is smaller than five fields of state spread across two closed objects.

B. Out-of-band negotiation (rejected). Keep prices in email/chat/sales calls and send a single final bid. Rejected because: the agreed price is not signed, not idempotent, and not replay-safe; it cannot be reconciled with post.pricing during a dispute (the audit is a mail archive); it gives an automated buyer no machine path, which is the entire requirement; and it carries consumer context into a channel with no PII boundary. The one thing it has — zero wire cost — is not worth an unattributable price.

C. Namespaced extension event values for the negotiation rounds (rejected). LEP-0007's route (open event values + details) is the right shape for a report, and it is the wrong shape for a typed price response: event.details is an open object whose content cannot be schema-constrained, so integer minor units, the floor echo, the round counter, and the allowlist could not be enforced — and an open details object is exactly the PII-smuggling shape this surface forbids. It would also make a state machine out of an event log, where "who may answer" is not expressible.

D. New ack.status values instead of rejection_reason (rejected). ack.status is a closed schema enum (schemas/ack.json) with a defined lifecycle; adding COUNTERED/EXPIRED_FOR_NEGOTIATION there is a receiver-visible closed-enum change on the one object every implementer switches on, and it would re-open money semantics (D2's whole argument is that status values near payability are dangerous). The existing open rejection_reason enumeration plus the new ack.negotiation.outcome closed enum (six values, scoped to negotiation acks only) carry the same information without touching the shared status axis.

E. Keep the behavior deployment-specific (rejected). A buyer agent that must implement each platform's bespoke counter endpoint has no interoperability value and no incentive to build it; the surface would fragment into N incompatible dialects, which is the failure mode LCP exists to prevent. D8 accepted the core cost for exactly this reason — while recording that the adoption evidence for the choice was waived, not produced.

Rollout, migration, observability, and rollback

Rollout.

  1. Schemas, vectors, OpenAPI, and SDK models land together (change gate I2); the feature is off.
  2. LCP_FEATURE_NEGOTIATION (default off, parsed in config.py alongside the other LCP_* switches) gates three things only: the features.negotiation advertisement, intake of the two message types, and token minting/redemption. With the flag off, the platform advertises message_types without the new values, answers LCP-006 for them, and writes no negotiation rows.
  3. Tables (negotiation, negotiation_round, negotiation_token) are created by initialize() unconditionally — a schema that depends on an env var is a migration hazard, and empty tables cost nothing. There is no backfill: nothing pre-existing maps onto a negotiation.
  4. Enable per-tenant/per-deployment only after the vectors and the platform suite are green (W5 gate row). Enabling is the advertisement change; no client upgrade is required on the peer side.
  5. A deployment that enables negotiation MUST advertise a lcp_versions element of 1.2.0 or higher, because features alone does not satisfy a strictly-conforming 1.2 sender's semver check.

Observability. The metrics above, plus the audit event stream; no consumer data and no token bytes in either. A dashboard per deployment should show rounds used (the cap's real cost), refusals by reason (the refusal path is the designed path, so it needs visibility, not paging), accepted→redeemed conversion (a low rate means the token TTL or ask is wrong), and expiry volume.

Rollback. Rollback is disabling the advertisement (the same mechanism OPERATIONS.md documents for the v1.1 slot surface): LCP_FEATURE_NEGOTIATION=off (or dropping the negotiation key and the 1.2.0 element from the advertisement) makes the surface unreachable for every peer, with no data-format migration.

  • In-flight negotiations become unusable: a redemption against them returns negotiation_closed. No money moves, because nothing was delivered — a negotiation is pre-delivery by construction.
  • Rows and audit trail are retained (non-PII, required for dispute reconstruction); they can be archived under W4-06 like any other non-PII history.
  • Already-redeemed tokens are ordinary bids and are unaffected by rollback.
  • Re-enabling is the same switch. There is no data epoch, no dual-write, and no SDK version pin, because the surface is opt-in in both directions.

Deferred questions

Each carries the trigger that reopens it. None of these blocks acceptance; all are recorded so a later change does not have to re-litigate this one.

# Question Why deferred Trigger to revisit
Q1 Should the protocol offer normative strategy (how many rounds to use, how to size a first counter)? Strategy is agent policy; a normative default would encode a commercial position into the core and make the platform's reserve guessable. Two adopters ship their own strategy and both ask for a shared default, with measured closed rates.
Q2 May a platform concede downward inside a negotiation (a real seller counter below its ask)? C6 forbids it: a lower counter is a price concession with no audit distinction from a mistake. Implementing it needs a monotone-rule revision (a signed delta, a concession flag, and a refusal reason for an unearned delta). Adopter evidence that restating the ask loses closable sales, plus a security review of the concession surface.
Q3 Should the buyer's ceiling travel on the wire (from offer.max_allowed_price_cents)? The ceiling is already offer-side configuration; putting it in a negotiation discloses the buyer's walk-away price to the seller. An adopter needs a wire-visible ceiling for cross-vendor automation, with a comparison rule and a refusal reason.
Q4 Negotiation over non-ping surfaces (post reprice, resale, subscription repricing). Different footing: post-delivery repricing touches payability, disputes, and already-delivered PII. Two independent adopters, plus a money-semantics review that starts from post.pricing, not from this record.
Q5 Multi-currency negotiation with FX comparison. D7 requires a non-stale, consistently-sourced rate before two prices may be compared; a negotiation would need that same rule plus a refusal reason for a stale rate. D7 has ≥2 adopters using fx_rate, and the negotiation refusal vocabulary gains a stale_rate value.
Q6 A read/list surface (GET /v1/lcp/negotiations, history parity with LEP-0005). No adopter has asked; it adds an authorization + pagination surface with a PII-adjacent audit read, and the state table is already queryable operationally. An operator needs self-service history, with a security review of the tenant predicate and a retention rule for the read.
Q7 max_rounds above the hard cap of 5. The cap is the abuse bound, not a tuning knob; raising it needs a measured benefit at the boundary. A deployment shows a closed-rate gain at 5 rounds that continues to 8, with cost data.
Q8 Negotiation for call leads (telephony pricing, per-minute terms). The same surface prices a lead; call pricing has its own unit (durations, payable_rules) and no evidence yet. Two adopters negotiating call pricing, plus a unit definition that is not minor units.
