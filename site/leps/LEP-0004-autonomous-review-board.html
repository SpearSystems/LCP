Problem statement and evidence

governance/LEP.md (Decision stage) requires that "a material proposal requires an independent second-maintainer review; security or privacy changes also require a named domain review," and AGENTS.md forbids anyone to "bypass independent review on behalf of maintainers." LCP has exactly one active maintainer. Every merge on origin/main to date — all 68 of them — was performed by that single person (verified at draft time with git log --merges --format='%an' origin/main | sort | uniq -c: two commit identities, one human, one machine). The independent-review clause is therefore already, in practice, a second hat on the same head, and its cost lands entirely on the owner's time:

  1. governance/LEP-REGISTRY.md shows all three numbered proposals (LEP-0001, LEP-0002, LEP-0003) blocked on "independent security/privacy review are pending" — the words appear on all three registry rows.
  2. The v1.1 program (docs/V1.1-ROADMAP.md) has produced a large volume of security- and privacy-relevant changes (signing canonicalization, slot-allocation PII flows, logging-hygiene gates, supply-chain pinning) whose review demand now exceeds the one human's throughput. The review labor, not the review authority, is the bottleneck.
  3. Owner-performed "independent" review of owner-authored changes is not independence by any reading of the clause; future external audits of the project (including the adversarial audits this proposal institutionalizes) will find the process itself non-compliant.

The fix is to redefine how the clause is satisfied, not to bypass it: an Autonomous Review Board (ARB) — a codified, adversarial, publicly re-runnable agent audit whose Review Dossier, when it meets the charter's quality gates, satisfies the independent-reviewer requirement, while the maintainer keeps the decision and the accountability. What is removed is the manual reading labor; what is added is machine-produced falsifiable evidence. The full normative mechanics (lanes, defect budget, gates, dissent, role separation) are the charter; this record proposes the governance change only.

Privacy, security, and universal-core audit

  • No PII boundary, authentication rule, retention rule, or lifecycle state changes. This proposal cannot leak data because it moves no data: dossiers cite commands, outputs, and file:lines of the public repository.
  • Universal-core audit (SPEC.md §12): no fields or message types are added; the audit is vacuous by scope.
  • The security surface of this proposal is the process itself, and the charter is written against the two ways a review process dies: rubber-stamping (the adversarial defect budget and judge gate — a PASS without ≥5 enumerated refutation attempts is void) and capture (everything — charter, prompts, template, dossiers — is public, and any adopter may re-run the harness at the same commit; a valid dissent blocks the decision until adjudicated).
  • Deliberate human anchor: proposals touching PII/auth/retention keep a named human reviewer or maintainer co-sign requirement (amendment above). For this proposal's own acceptance, the chicken-and-egg clause in ARB.md applies and is disclosed plainly: LEP-0004 cannot be reviewed by the machinery it creates; the sole maintainer accepts it, if at all, as a recorded process exception whose independent control is external re-runnability plus the public 14-day window. No future proposal may invoke this clause.

Alternatives considered

  1. Recruit a second human maintainer. Cleanest legitimacy. No candidate exists today, and nothing prevents the same requirement being met this way later — the amendment is "satisfied by", not "only by". Rejected for now for lack of candidates; remains always available.
  2. Keep owner-only review. The status quo; does not satisfy the clause's intent (independence), does not satisfy it at scale, and blocks three proposals indefinitely.
  3. Silence-is-consent. ARB-PASS + 14-day window expiry auto-Accepts unless the owner objects. Fastest, but it moves a PII protocol's legal and privacy decisions into automation-by-default and lets unreviewed decisions accrue on maintainer inattention. Explicitly rejected as the adoption model (kept as a deferred question with a trigger below).
  4. Human-ARB hybrid (ARB pre-reads, human reviews everything). Removes none of the bottleneck; ARB evidence then exists only as an advisory digest. Rejected as paying the cost without the accountability win.
  5. Proposed: ARB dossier satisfies the independent-review requirement under charter gates, with per-decision maintainer sign-off and the PII/auth/retention human anchor.

Rollout, migration, observability, and rollback

  1. Acceptance → apply the amendment above to governance/LEP.md in a dedicated implementation PR linking this record and the registry row; registry checker must pass; this LEP's status transition is recorded per the chicken-and-egg clause.
  2. First runs → LEP-0002 and LEP-0003 receive full lane fan-outs and judge-ratified dossiers posted to issues #121/#124 (their 14-day public windows run in parallel as before); owner signs each decision from the dossier with one reply.
  3. Steady state → every material LEP decision requires a dossier at the decision SHA; dissent re-runs are open to any adopter continuously.
  4. Observability → metrics recorded on the discussion issues: dossier turnaround (target <48h from request), dissent count, findings converted to CI tests per lane, and maintainer overrides of ARB verdicts (a maintainer MAY override — the decision was always theirs — and the override must be stated in the decision rationale).
  5. Rollback → revert the LEP.md amendment PR. Recorded decisions survive untouched (they were always maintainer decisions citing evidence), and the charter/dossier history remains as public audit trail. No migration or compatibility window is needed because nothing external consumes this process.

Deferred questions

  • Silence-is-consent automation — rejected by this proposal's adoption model, kept as a deferred question. Trigger to revisit: after ≥5 complete ARB decision cycles with zero maintainer overrides of judge-ratified dossiers AND at least two independent external adopters having re-run the harness at published SHAs, the owner may reconsider auto-accept for non-PII/non-auth/non-retention proposals only.
  • Model monoculture — one model family auditing code generated by the same family shares blind spots; charter makes cross-model judge SHOULD, not MUST. Trigger to revisit: first dissent or finding class that a second model family demonstrably catches and the first missed.
  • ARB review of ARB changes — edits to the charter itself are material governance changes; who audits the auditors' manual is unresolved beyond dissent + public window. Trigger to revisit: first proposed charter amendment.
  • Third-party hosted re-runs — adopters without local harness capacity rely on maintainers publishing invocation instructions. Trigger to revisit: ≥2 adopters requesting a hosted re-run facility.
