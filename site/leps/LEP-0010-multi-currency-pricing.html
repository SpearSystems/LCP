Problem statement and evidence

Cross-border lead generation is the commercial case: a US buyer purchasing Australian mortgage leads, or an AU platform reselling to a UK call centre. LCP prices everything in integer minor units of one currency and gives the auction no way to express a second one.

What the protocol has today. ping.floor_price_cents + ping.currency, bid.bid_price_cents + bid.currency, and post.price_cents + post.currency (spec/02-envelope.md:103-104 for ping, :135-136 for bid, :170-174 for post); currency is schema-required on ping and post (schemas/ping.json:13-14, schemas/post.json:10-11) and required on bid only when decision = accept (schemas/bid.json:62-67). There is no rate, no native-amount field, and no notion of settlement currency distinct from the quoted currency.

What that costs, measured. The reference platform's auction loop compares bids to the offer only when the strings are equal, and otherwise drops the candidate silently:

# implementations/reference-platform/lcp_platform/router.py (pre-D7 shape)
if bid["bid_price_cents"] < offer["floor_price_cents"]:
    continue
if bid["currency"] != offer["currency"]:
    continue            # ← a valid AUD bid against a USD offer simply vanishes

A cross-currency bid is therefore not "unsupported with an explanation"; it is invisible. The buyer receives RECEIVED (router.py submit_bid, ack status RECEIVED), hears nothing further — which is also what a losing bid hears (spec/02-envelope.md:145, "losers receive no further messages") — and cannot tell a currency refusal from an auction loss. A platform operator cannot count the refusals either: no audit row, no reason, no metric.

What the protocol already knows how to do. FINAL_PRICING events carry winning_price_cents in integer minor units plus currency, with a typo guard (spec/02-envelope.md:266) and an explicit settlement-neutrality rule that this LEP reuses verbatim for analytics: a pricing report must never re-price a live auction or move a payable (spec/02-envelope.md:278-292). Prices are already integer minor units everywhere, so the missing piece is not a unit change — it is a second currency plus the metadata that lets two of them be compared honestly.

Why "just convert it" is not a design. Minor units are not comparable across currencies (100 JPY and 100 USD differ by ~150×), minor units are not even comparable across the same pair when the ISO 4217 minor-unit exponents differ (USD has 2, JPY has 0), and a rate is a time-bounded observation from a named source, not a constant. Any of the three handled by guesswork produces a charge nobody can reconstruct.

Scope

  • Core protocol (additive MINOR): six optional fields inside post.pricing (currency, original_price_cents, fx_rate, fx_source, fx_timestamp, fx_expires_at) and the four fx_* fields on bid; one normative ranking rule in SPEC §8; one normative settlement/rounding rule; one exclusion-reason vocabulary on the routing-decision surface. message.type is untouched (closed enum, spec/05-capabilities.md:32), no new envelope shape, no new LCP-* code.
  • SPEC text (split documents, D10): spec/03-payloads.md §3, spec/02-envelope.md §4 (bid field table + post pricing table), spec/04-errors.md §5 (no new code; the mapping table below), spec/05-capabilities.md §8 (features.multi_currency advertisement and the comparability rule).
  • Reference implementation: LCP_FX_MAX_AGE_SECONDS (default 300), LCP_FEATURE_FX_PRICING (default off), the comparability/exclusion path in the auction, the bid.non_comparable audit row, and the post-side FX echo. Enumerated in Impact.
  • Not in scope: a platform-issued reference rate or any rate service; a registered list of FX providers; triangulation between more than one source in one auction; FX-aware quote/counter negotiation (LEP-0009); per-market accounting or tax rounding rules; refund/chargeback FX policy; multi-currency quotas or monthly minimums on offers.
  • Deliberately unchanged: the offer's floor_price_cents + currency stay the auction's single settlement basis (schemas/offer.json, required). A platform does not gain a second settlement currency per offer; it gains honest handling of bidders and buyers who price in another one.

Proposed wire shape

1. Effective settlement currency (normative resolution ladder)

The settlement currency is the currency the platform charges, invoices, and pays out in. It is resolved once per price, in this order:

  1. post.pricing.currency, when present;
  2. otherwise post.currency (the payload-level field, required by schemas/post.json:10-11 and populated from the offer by the reference platform, messages.py:223);
  3. otherwise USD.

For a bid, the settlement currency is the ping's offer currency (offer.currency, required), and a platform that cannot resolve it falls back to USD — the reference platform's existing default (router.py:1205-1208: str(currency or "USD").upper()).

Reconciliation with the registry row. The registry row says "absent currency means USD". That is step 3 and it is the terminal default. It cannot be step 1: post.currency is required, and the shipped example posts a lead at price_cents: 2200, currency: "AUD" (examples/post.json:18-19). If an absent pricing.currency overrode a present payload.currency, every existing post would be silently re-denominated from AUD to USD the day a reader adopts this LEP. Step 2 exists so that adopting the field changes no existing document's meaning; step 3 exists so a payload carrying neither (schema-invalid today, but seen in the wild) is read rather than guessed. A v1.0/v1.1 post keeps its current settlement currency under this ladder.

When both post.pricing.currency and post.currency are present and differ, the payload is malformed: the receiver MUST reject it (LCP-100 VALIDATION_ERROR, field: "pricing.currency"). Two settlement currencies in one billing record is exactly the ambiguity this LEP exists to remove; picking one would be coercion.

2. post.pricing — field table

All six fields are optional. A v1.0/v1.1 sender emits none of them and is unaffected (see Compatibility, versioning, and deprecation impact). Types below are what schemas/post.json:48-129 must carry (additionalProperties: false, so anything not listed is a schema rejection).

Field Type Constraint Required when
currency string ISO 4217 alpha-3, ^[A-Z]{3}$ (the existing core.json#/$defs/currency, schemas/core.json:499-501). MUST equal post.currency when that is present. MUST NOT be the native currency when a conversion occurred — this field is the settlement currency, always. Optional. Absent resolves per §1's ladder (post.currency, else USD), which keeps v1.0/v1.1 payloads at their current settlement currency. SHOULD be present whenever any fx_* field is present.
original_price_cents integer minimum: 0. Native minor units of the winning bid's bid.currency (see §3). REQUIRED when any fx_* field is present; MUST be absent when no conversion occurred (there is no "original" on a single-currency post).
fx_rate string (decimal) Plain decimal, no sign, no exponent, no grouping, no leading-zero integer part, a mandatory fractional part of 6–12 digits, value in [0.000001, 1000000]. Normative pattern in 4.1. Direction: native major units per 1 settlement major unit. REQUIRED when the award converted a cross-currency bid; MUST be absent otherwise.
fx_source string ^(bilateral|[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]{0,63})$. Named rate provider, or the literal bilateral for a rate the two parties agreed directly. Compared by exact byte equality; case is normalised by the pattern, never by the receiver. REQUIRED when fx_rate is present.
fx_timestamp string (date-time) Canonical whole-second UTC: YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ, exactly one Z, no fractional part, no offset (the LEP-0002 canonical form, spec/01-transport.md:51-59). The instant the rate was observed. REQUIRED when fx_rate is present.
fx_expires_at string (date-time) Same canonical form. MUST be strictly later than fx_timestamp. Absolute end of the rate's validity. REQUIRED when fx_rate is present.

Why the native amount is not self-describing (known limitation). original_price_cents is denominated in the winning bid's bid.currency, which the receiving buyer sent and therefore holds, so the buyer can always recompute the charge. A third party reading only the post — a publisher, an auditor, a dispute reviewer — cannot decode the native currency from the post alone, because D7's field list carries no native-currency code. This LEP does not add a seventh field (the decision fixed the list); the gap is recorded as deferred question Q-1 with a concrete trigger, and until then the platform MUST be able to answer it from its own bid record.

3. bid — field table (the price side that feeds the auction)

Four optional fields, flat on the bid payload (bid has no pricing block; the existing price fields are flat, schemas/bid.json:25-31):

Field Type Constraint Required when
currency (existing) string ISO 4217 alpha-3 (existing). Semantics refined, not changed: this is the currency the bid is expressed in — i.e. the native currency of bid_price_cents. In v1.0/v1.1 it necessarily equalled the offer's currency; from v1.2 a bid MAY differ from it. REQUIRED when decision = "accept" (existing condition, schemas/bid.json:62-67).
bid_price_cents (existing) integer minimum: 0. Native minor units of bid.currency. REQUIRED when decision = "accept" (existing).
fx_rate string (decimal) As post.pricing.fx_rate, same direction and same pattern. REQUIRED when bid.currency differs from the offer's currency; MUST NOT be relied on otherwise.
fx_source string As post.pricing.fx_source. REQUIRED with fx_rate.
fx_timestamp string (date-time) As post.pricing.fx_timestamp. REQUIRED with fx_rate.
fx_expires_at string (date-time) As post.pricing.fx_expires_at; MUST be strictly later than fx_timestamp. REQUIRED with fx_rate.

No original_price_cents on the bid. On a bid the native amount is bid_price_cents; a second copy of the same number in the same message is a divergence waiting to happen. The post echoes it once (§2, original_price_cents) so the delivered billing record carries both sides of the conversion.

A same-currency bid ignores its FX fields (normative). When bid.currency equals the offer's settlement currency, the bid compares directly by integer minor units and any fx_* metadata present is inert and MUST be ignored for comparison (it is still schema-validated). No conversion happens, so no coefficient can be wrong; rejecting the message instead would punish a buyer that sends a uniform envelope. The reference platform already behaves this way (router.py:1211-1212 returns the amount before reading metadata).

Rates and amounts are covered by the existing envelope HMAC and replay window — no new authentication surface, no new secret.

4. Rate semantics and conversion arithmetic

4.1 The rate is a plain decimal with declared precision

fx_rate is native major units per 1 settlement major unit. AUD per USD is 1.520000; USD per JPY is 0.00636943 (≈ 157 JPY per USD).

Tightened normative pattern (supersedes the looser shape currently landed in schemas/post.json:79-81 and the reference platform's _FX_RATE_RE, router.py:167-170):

^(?:[1-9][0-9]*\.[0-9]{6,12}|0\.[0-9]*[1-9][0-9]{5,11})$

The pattern rejects, and a receiver MUST reject with LCP-100 VALIDATION_ERROR / field: "fx_rate": an exponent ("1.52e0"), a comma decimal separator ("1,52"), a sign, whitespace, a missing fractional part ("2"), zero ("0"), a leading-zero integer part ("007.000000"), fewer than six fractional digits for a value ≥ 1 ("1.52"), and fewer than six significant fractional digits for a value < 1 ("0.000123"). Trailing zeros are permitted and are read as declared quantization, not as extra precision.

Measured gap in the tree as it stands. The landed schema pattern ^[0-9]+(\.[0-9]{6,})?$ accepts "1", "0", "007.000000" and "0.000123" (verified by validating the example payloads of this LEP against schemas/post.json with jsonschema 4.26.0 from the repository .venv; each of those four values passes the landed pattern). The tightening is an implementation obligation, not an optional polish: an integer-only rate and a zero rate are the two shapes a hand-rolled writer produces first.

Bounds: value in [0.000001, 1000000] (all ISO 4217 fiat crosses fall inside it), fractional digits ≤ 12. Twelve digits plus the 100000000 minor-unit ceiling already used for typo-guarded prices (spec/02-envelope.md:266) keeps exact arithmetic cheap, but an implementation MUST NOT rely on 64-bit integer cross-multiplication of unscaled operands — scale deliberately or use arbitrary-precision decimal. The reference platform uses Python Decimal (router.py:1244).

4.2 Minor-unit exponents are load-bearing

original_price_cents / bid_price_cents are minor units; fx_rate is a major-unit quote. Converting therefore needs the ISO 4217 minor-unit exponent of both currencies:

settlement_minor_exact = native_minor × 10^(exp_settlement − exp_native) ÷ fx_rate

Worked, with the exponents pinned (USD = 2, AUD = 2, JPY = 0):

  • native_minor 50000 USD (USD 500.00) → settlement JPY at fx_rate "0.00636943": 50000 × 10^(0−2) ÷ 0.00636943 = 78499.959965… JPY → charge 78500 JPY (half-up, §7).
  • The same division without the exponent factor yields 7849995.96 — a 100× overcharge. This is the failure mode the rule forbids.

A platform MUST carry the ISO 4217 exponent table it uses, and MUST treat a currency whose exponent it cannot resolve as fx_currency_malformed / fx_settlement_currency_malformed rather than assuming 2. The reference platform has no exponent table today and divides minor units directly (router.py:1244); it is correct only for same-exponent pairs (AUD/USD, USD/EUR) and is wrong for USD/JPY, USD/KRW, USD/ISK and every 0- or 3-exponent currency. Blocking test T-EXPONENT covers it.

4.3 Exact comparison, never a rounded or clamped value

Every comparability decision uses the exact decimal value of settlement_minor_exact. No float, no bank rounding, no clamping to the floor or to the guardrail. Decimal("2200.5") < Decimal("2200.6") decides the auction; 2201 does not.

5. Comparability rule (normative)

C1. Two prices MUST NOT be compared unless their settlement currencies are the same, or each is accompanied by an FX bundle that is present, well-formed, non-stale, non-expired, and consistently sourced.

C2. "Present" means all four of fx_rate, fx_source, fx_timestamp, fx_expires_at are non-empty on the candidate.

C3. "Non-stale" is evaluated against one evaluation_time shared by every candidate of one auction evaluation (a single routing sweep), not per candidate: evaluation_time − fx_timestamp ≤ LCP_FX_MAX_AGE_SECONDS (default 300, inclusive, matching the replay-window convention of SEC/ERR-016) — and evaluation_time < fx_expires_at. A fx_timestamp more than LCP_FX_MAX_AGE_SECONDS in the future relative to evaluation_time is also stale (clock-skew guard, same reason string). fx_expires_at MAY be shorter than the max-age window and MUST NOT be read as extending it.

C4. "Consistently sourced" means every converted candidate in one evaluation carries the same fx_source value, byte for byte. If the converted candidates of one evaluation carry two or more distinct values, all converted candidates in that evaluation are non-comparable (fx_source_mixed) and only same-currency candidates remain. The platform MUST NOT pick one source, average sources, or triangulate.

C5. A non-comparable offer MUST be excluded with the reason string from §6, recorded, and left otherwise untouched: no default rate, no assumed parity, no "rate of 1", no clamping to the floor, no rewriting of the bid payload. Excluding with an explanation is reversible and auditable; guessing a rate is not (D7 rationale).

C6. A non-comparable offer MUST NOT fail the auction. If every candidate is excluded, the existing "no candidates → expire the lead" path runs unchanged. A same-currency candidate is never affected by another candidate's FX metadata.

C7. Format-invalid FX values (pattern, timestamp form, source shape) are an intake fault where the platform can see them at intake (LCP-100, §6), and a comparison-time exclusion otherwise — because the feature flag can be enabled while bids are already stored, and stored bids are never re-validated. Both paths use the same reason strings.

C8. The auction's ordering key is the exact settlement value, then the existing deterministic tie-breaks (estimated_contact_seconds, received_at, buyer_id — router.py:1359-1367 in the current tree). FX adds no new tie-break and changes no existing one.

6. Exclusion reasons and error-code mapping

The reason strings below are the vocabulary the platform emits. They are documented known values of the open rejection_reason enumeration (schemas/ack.json:44, $comment: "Open enum") and, at comparison time, the reason of a bid.non_comparable audit row (router.py:1246-1261: action="bid.non_comparable", with lead_id, offer_id, buyer_id, currency, reason). No new LCP-* code is allocated and no schema enum changes: the open-enum mechanism carries the meaning, exactly as LEP-0007's ignored did.

Reason Emitted when Intake code (where detectable at intake)
fx_rate_missing a cross-currency candidate carries no complete FX bundle (C2) LCP-100 VALIDATION_ERROR, 400, field: "fx_rate"
fx_rate_stale evaluation_time − fx_timestamp > LCP_FX_MAX_AGE_SECONDS, or the timestamp is too far in the future (C3) LCP-100, 400, field: "fx_timestamp"
fx_rate_expired evaluation_time ≥ fx_expires_at LCP-100, 400, field: "fx_expires_at" — a rate already expired on arrival can never be used

Notes that keep the vocabulary honest:

  • price_below_floor is required for converted candidates. Today the floor test is a silent continue for every candidate (router.py:1324-1325); for a converted candidate it MUST be recorded with this reason, because "your AUD bid was below a USD floor after conversion" is exactly the surprise this LEP removes. Same-currency floor exclusions MAY keep the existing silent behaviour (no regression is introduced either way).
  • LCP-014 is not allocated. docs/V1.2-EXECUTION-PLAN.md:111 (W1-09) offers "stale rate → LCP-014 (or documented existing code)". This record chooses the documented existing code: a stale rate is a value-validity verdict on an authenticated message, and LCP-100 VALIDATION_ERROR is the code the platform already uses for "validation rejection, not a store" (spec/02-envelope.md:228, :276; spec/04-errors.md:24). Allocating LCP-014 for one of eleven reasons would leave the other ten mapping to nothing; the reason string, not the code, carries the diagnosis.
  • LCP-001 INVALID_FORMAT stays the request-framing code (bad JSON, wrong content type, oversized body — service.py:79-110) and is not used for a price-value verdict.

7. Rounding, settlement, and what is payable

One rounding event of record. The auction ranks on settlement_minor_exact (§4.3). When a cross-currency candidate wins, the charge is computed once, at award, as the half-up rounding of that exact value to an integer minor unit ((x + 0.5).floor for x ≥ 0; prices are non-negative by schema, so the negative-half-up ambiguity does not arise):

Exact settlement value Charge of record (half-up)
220000 220000
2200.5 2201
78499.959965… 78500

That integer is post.pricing.final_price_cents and post.price_cents, it is written once, and it is immutable: a later rate, a later auction sweep, a re-ranking, or a retry MUST NOT recompute or adjust it. The reference platform already rounds exactly there, after ranking (router.py:1388-1390), with the comment "the rounded cents never feed back into ranking".

Rounding can collapse an order but must not create one. Half-up is monotone, so it can never invert a strict order — and it can turn one into a tie: candidates at exact 2200.5 and 2200.6 both round to 2201, so a ranking performed on rounded values would lose the winner that the exact values distinguish. The auction therefore uses exact values (C8), and rounding is confined to the charge.

Rounding must not admit a sub-floor bid. Exact 149999.666… (native 449999 AUD cents at fx_rate "3.000000") is below a 150000 USD-cent floor even though half-up would present it as 150000. The floor test uses the exact value; the bid is excluded with price_below_floor (§6) and no charge is created.

A converted amount that cannot be expressed is excluded, not rounded to zero. If settlement_minor_exact < 0.5, the platform MUST exclude the candidate (price_below_floor when a floor exists, otherwise the same reason with the audit note unrepresentable) rather than record a 0 charge. A zero charge is a commercial fact (a free lead), never a rounding artifact.

What is payable. The settlement integer in pricing.final_price_cents / price_cents, governed by the existing payable_definition, payable_status, and dispute window (spec/02-envelope.md:170-174, schemas/post.json:48-129). post.pricing remains "the sole billing and dispute record between platform and buyer" (spec/02-envelope.md:278). Nothing in this LEP changes when a lead becomes payable, what a dispute window measures, or how an ack interacts with either.

What is analytics. original_price_cents, all four fx_* values, and any FINAL_PRICING event. They are the conversion's evidence: the buyer and the platform can both recompute settlement_minor_exact from them, so a charge can always be reconstructed and a dispute argued without trusting a log. They are not re-inputs: no later FX movement re-prices a settled lead, and a FINAL_PRICING report — which may name a different currency entirely — stays analytics and bid refinement only (spec/02-envelope.md:278-292). Post-award FX drift is the platform's commercial risk and MUST NOT appear as a payable adjustment without a separate, explicitly-recorded agreement.

Privacy, security, and universal-core audit

PII. No consumer data enters any field in this LEP. Classes follow docs/PII-CLASSES.md (D9): pricing.currency is public ("a code, not a commercial fact", PII-CLASSES.md:680), and every amount, rate, source, and timestamp is internal (PII-CLASSES.md:681-685). The bid-side fx_* fields are internal and need rows in that table when schemas/bid.json gains them. Nothing here is eligible for a ping: the ping allowlist is unchanged, so LCP-008 PII_IN_PING keeps its boundary. No consumer identifier is joined to a rate, so aggressive FX analytics cannot become a re-identification surface.

Money integrity. The rate is self-attested. A buyer that supplies its own rate can shape its own converted bid, and bilateral rates are by definition unverifiable. This LEP makes that visible rather than silent — the rate, source, and timestamp travel with the price and are recorded in the billing record — but it does not make it safe: a platform that needs rate integrity MUST pin fx_source (refuse bilateral, name one provider) and SHOULD reject a rate that deviates from its own reference beyond a tolerance it defines. Neither mechanism is normative here; both are deferred (Q-2, Q-3).

Griefing, stated plainly. C4's rule is mutual: a candidate cannot gain by carrying a novel fx_source, because it excludes itself too — but it can exclude a rival, and in a two-converted-candidate auction both vanish and a same-currency bidder wins by default. The exposure is bounded (the attacker never wins on the tricked axis) and the mitigation is the same pinning above. It is recorded, not hidden (Q-3).

Transport and authorization. No new endpoint, scope, secret, or key type. The bid is HMAC-signed over the payload, so rate tampering in flight is already covered by LCP-012; replay by LCP-005/idempotency. The audit row is written with the platform as actor and carries no consumer data.

Universal-core audit. The fields work for: an AU publisher selling to a US buyer (AUD native, USD settlement); a USD buyer into an AUD offer (the reverse, same arithmetic); a JPY-settling deployment (0-exponent minor unit, §4.2); a deployment with no FX provider at all (bilateral); a deployment that pins one source (C4 with a single member); and v1.0/v1.1 peers, which never see the fields. No field names a bank, a provider, a jurisdiction, a tax regime, or a payment rail. The only external dependency is ISO 4217 itself, and its minor-unit exponents are part of the standard, not a vendor.

Compatibility, versioning, and deprecation impact

  • Additive MINOR. Every field is optional in both directions. A v1.2 sender addressing a v1.1 receiver and a v1.1 sender addressing a v1.2 receiver both keep working, in the second case with no FX metadata at all — which is exactly the pre-v1.2 behaviour.
  • The one real hazard is the closed pricing object. It is additionalProperties: false (schemas/post.json:129), so a post that newly populates pricing.currency toward a v1.1 receiver is a schema rejection (LCP-100). Senders therefore MUST gate emission on the receiver's advertisement: capabilities().lcp_versions containing a parsed major == 1, minor >= 2 element and capabilities().features.multi_currency present. This is the LEP-0003 §4 gate verbatim, and the reference platform already advertises the key (router.py:2387-2389: "multi_currency": "1.2.0") while its lcp_versions constant still lists only 1.0.0/1.1.0 (validation.py:23) — a blocking gap, T-CAPGATE.
  • Bid side is the opposite case: schemas/bid.json:71 is also closed, so the fields are simply not accepted today. Nothing is redefined; bid.currency keeps its meaning (the currency of bid_price_cents) and merely stops being forced to equal the offer's.
  • post.currency semantics are unchanged. It remains the payload's price currency and, from v1.2, the settlement currency. §1's ladder guarantees no existing document changes meaning.
  • No deprecation, no removal, no N+2 window needed. Nothing in this LEP supersedes a field. The spec/00-conformance.md:162 deferred row is resolved by this record and the roadmap row moves to implemented on ship.
  • Version string. lcp.version continues to identify the core schema line; the capability advertisement, not the version literal, is what a sender gates on (D3's rule, DECISIONS-v1.2.md D3).

Alternatives considered

  1. Leave it deployment-specific / bilateral (no fields). Cheap and zero-wire. Rejected: the auction can neither compare nor explain, and the delivered billing record cannot show what was converted. The current silent drop (router.py pre-D7 continue) is the status quo this LEP replaces with an auditable exclusion.
  2. A new fx_quote message type. Rejected: message.type is closed (spec/05-capabilities.md:32), so every v1.0/v1.1 receiver rejects it with LCP-006, and price metadata would acquire its own lifecycle instead of travelling with the price it describes.
  3. Convert at the publisher and quote buyers in their own currency. Rejected: it moves FX risk to the party with the least information, and it cannot express a multi-buyer auction — one publisher price is in one currency, while the buyers are in several.
  4. Store the rate as a JSON number (float). Rejected outright: float money is the failure D7 forbids, 1e-7 style literals are already treated as a sentinel in this repository (implementations/reference-platform/lcp_platform/attestation.py:201), and only a string can carry declared, byte-exact precision.
  5. Express the rate as minor-per-minor. Tempting: it removes §4.2's exponent factor entirely. Rejected: the number would no longer be the quote a human or provider supplies, so every rate would need an undocumented adjustment at the edge — a class of silent 100× errors traded for one explicit multiplication.
  6. Have the platform publish the rate and convert everything centrally. Attractive (one source, verifiable, no self-attestation) but out of scope: it needs a rate service, an SLA, a quote surface, and a policy for who bears drift. Deferred as Q-2 with a real trigger.
  7. Reject cross-currency bids outright (today's effect, made explicit). Rejected: it forbids the exact use case and converts a data problem into a hard commercial limit.
  8. Provider-signed rate attestations. Reuses LEP-0008 machinery, but requires key distribution and a provider ecosystem that does not exist. Deferred (Q-3).

Rollout, migration, observability, and rollback

Rollout. Ship the schema/SDK/OpenAPI/MCP surfaces and the vectors with the flag off; enable in CI. capabilities().features.multi_currency is advertised only when the behaviour is actually enabled in that build (the advertisement is a promise, not a plan version). Senders gate on the advertisement plus a parsed minor >= 2 (see Compatibility, versioning, and deprecation impact).

Migration. None. Stored bids have no FX metadata, so a cross-currency stored bid is non-comparable exactly as it is today; a same-currency stored bid is untouched. No backfill, no rewrite, no dual-write. Posts already delivered keep their meaning under §1's ladder. Databases need no new columns: the FX fields live in the stored envelope, and the audit row uses the existing audit table.

Observability. fx_excluded_total{reason} (one series per reason in §6), fx_converted_total, fx_settlement_charge_minor{currency} for reconciliation, and the bid.non_comparable audit row (router.py:1246-1261) for case-level forensics. Dashboards need two panels: exclusion rate by reason, and converted-award count versus same-currency awards. No PII in any of them.

Rollback. Turn LCP_FEATURE_FX_PRICING off. With the flag off the platform does not accept the bid-side fields, does not convert, does not write bid.non_comparable, and does not echo FX fields on posts — i.e. today's behaviour, including the silent skip for a cross-currency bid. Because the post fields are optional, already-emitted posts stay valid documents; nothing has to be un-emitted, re-computed, or back-filled. A revert of the schema files is not required for a rollback and MUST NOT be baked into one.
