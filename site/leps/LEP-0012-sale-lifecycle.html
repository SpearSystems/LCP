LEP-0012 — Sale lifecycle: lead disposition, money events, URL postbacks, and test outcomes

  • Title: Sale lifecycle: synchronous lead disposition, money-bearing lifecycle events, URL-template postbacks, tracking passthrough, and defined test outcomes
  • Author(s): LCP maintainers (draft from SpearPointX adopter evidence)
  • Date: 2026-11-19
  • Status: Review — public review open until 2026-12-03. Not accepted. A candidate implementation is on branch lep/0012-0013-sale-lifecycle; it does not merge until this record is Accepted (LEP.md stage 5).
  • Target version: v1.3.0 (additive MINOR: one optional object on the closed ack payload, one optional property on subscription, documented details shapes on three existing event names, two registered extensions; no new message type, no new error code)
  • Discussion: #254
  • Reviewers needed at Review: at least one publisher, one buyer, and one lead network (domain); a named human security/privacy reviewer, because §4 adds an outbound HTTP surface and §1 carries a session-bearing URL.
  • Companion records: LEP-0013 (regulated identifiers) and LEP-0014 (personal_loans vertical). Each is decidable on its own.

Problem statement and evidence

What senders need back from a lead post

A publisher or network that posts a lead needs four facts, at post time:

  1. Did it sell?
  2. For how much, so ad spend can be optimised on revenue rather than on form fills.
  3. Where to send the consumer next, when the buyer requires a browser handoff.
  4. What changed later: a pending lead resolving, or a sold lead returned.

Real buyer APIs already answer the first three synchronously. A US personal-loans buyer's v2 lead API, used by the reference deployment, answers every post with:

  • a status: accepted, rejected, or rejected on price with an alternative price;
  • the price, as a decimal string;
  • a consumer redirect_url that the buyer measures as a redirect rate.

Ping-tree buyers and lead networks commonly do the same.

What LCP has today

  • ack.status is message-level only. It reports ACCEPTED, REJECTED and so on for the message. It has no sale outcome, no price, and no redirect. ack.json is closed (additionalProperties: false), so a receiver cannot add them without breaking strict validators.
  • Events carry no money for a sale or a return. CONVERTED and FINAL_PRICING have money shapes. ACCEPTED, REJECTED and REFUNDED have none, so a sender cannot reconcile a return against an earlier sale.
  • Subscriptions are JSON webhooks only (LEP-0001). Publishers optimise ads through affiliate trackers. Those trackers accept server-to-server postbacks as an HTTPS GET with values substituted into a URL template. They do not run an HMAC-verifying JSON receiver, and nor do no-code form stacks.
  • The test flag has a traffic policy (LCP-013) but no defined outcome. An integrator cannot make a test post come back sold, unsold or pending, so the branches that matter most go untested until live traffic.

What it costs, measured in the reference deployment

  • SpearPointX had to plan a non-LCP response body (outcome, payout, redirect) for publishers. That is a protocol fork in the first adopter.
  • A publisher integration guide converted dollars to cents twice. Nothing in the response told the publisher what the lead earned, so the error could only surface at statement time.
  • Publisher test posts can only prove that the request validates, not that the integration handles a sale.

Evidence status

There is one adopter deployment, one documented buyer API, and the industry-standard S2S postback convention. The two-adopter rule in LEP.md applies to new message types. This record adds none: every change is an optional field, a documented details shape, or a registered extension.

Scope

  • Core protocol (additive MINOR):

    • ack.disposition: an optional object on the ack of a lead or post (§1);
    • subscription.delivery: an optional property selecting JSON or URL-template delivery (§4).
  • Documented event shapes: details for ACCEPTED, REJECTED and REFUNDED when they report a sale result to the sender of the lead (§3).

  • Registered extensions:

    • lcp.affiliate.tracking: click and sub-ID passthrough (§5);
    • lcp.test.outcome: a forced test outcome (§6).
  • Capability advertisement: features.sale_disposition, features.url_postbacks and features.test_outcomes, each naming 1.3.0.
  • Normative text: consumer redirect handling (§2) and test-outcome behaviour (§6).

  • Not in scope:

    • settlement, invoicing, or the publisher statement of account;
    • pricing strategy;
    • a price floor on a direct lead post (deferred, see Deferred questions);
    • consumer-facing redirect pages;
    • shared-lead (LEP-0003) multi-buyer dispositions.

Proposed wire shape

1. ack.disposition

disposition is an optional object on the payload of an ack whose original_message_id names a lead or post. It reports the commercial result. ack.status keeps reporting message processing.

Field Type Rule
outcome string, closed enum sold, unsold, pending, price_rejected. Required.
payout_cents integer, 1–100000000 Amount the receiver will pay the sender for this lead, in minor units of currency, after any receiver fee. Required when outcome = sold; absent otherwise. Same typo guard as FINAL_PRICING.winning_price_cents.
currency string, ISO 4217 Required when payout_cents or counter_price_cents is present.
counter_price_cents integer, 1–100000000 price_rejected only: the lowest price the receiver would pay. Informational in v1.3.0 (see Deferred questions).
redirect_url string, ^https://, ≤ 2048 sold only, optional. The consumer's next page. Classed restricted (§2).
redirect_expires_at string, date-time Optional, with redirect_url only.
final_by string, date-time pending only, optional: the latest time the final event will be sent.

Status and disposition together (normative):

  • A receiver includes disposition only on an ack with status = RECEIVED, ACCEPTED or DUPLICATE. A receiver that routes asynchronously answers RECEIVED with outcome = pending (or with a forced test outcome, §6).
  • A DUPLICATE ack for an idempotent replay MUST repeat the disposition of the original ack, byte for byte, when one was issued. Retries then learn the result instead of losing it.
  • rejection_reason, the existing open enum, MAY accompany outcome = unsold or price_rejected. Its description widens from "when status = REJECTED" to "when the lead was refused or not bought".
  • An ack with status = REJECTED and no disposition means the receiver refused the lead. A sender treats it as unsold.
  • Sender rule: a lead is sold if, and only if, disposition.outcome = sold. A sender MUST NOT infer a sale from HTTP 2xx or from status = ACCEPTED alone.

Pending. The final result arrives as an ACCEPTED (sold) or REJECTED (unsold) event (§3), by subscription or through GET /v1/lcp/events. final_by bounds the wait. After final_by with no event, the sender treats the lead as unsold, and a later ACCEPTED event still supersedes that by revision.

Version gate. ack.json is closed, so a strict 1.2 sender would reject an ack that carries disposition. A receiver MUST include disposition only when the acknowledged message's lcp.version is 1.3.0 or later, parsed as semver and never compared as a string. A 1.2 sender therefore never receives it.

2. Consumer redirect (normative)

The receiver measures the redirect from the consumer's own client: their IP address, user agent and cookies. The rules below keep that measurement honest.

  • The sender MUST deliver redirect_url to the consumer's browser as a top-level navigation: an HTTP 302/303 from the sender's page, or a script navigation. It SHOULD do so immediately after the ack.
  • The sender MUST NOT request the URL from a server. It MUST NOT prefetch or preload it, render it in an iframe, or pass it to a link-preview service. A server-side request records a false redirect from the wrong client.

  • A receiver MAY wrap a downstream receiver's URL in its own relay hop (https://relay.example/r/{token} → 302 → downstream URL), which lets it measure redirect rate per sender. A relay:

    • MUST use an opaque, unguessable token with no consumer data;
    • MUST be HTTPS;
    • MUST answer 404 for an unknown token;
    • SHOULD answer a repeat hit before redirect_expires_at with the same destination;
    • MUST NOT add query parameters derived from consumer data.

  • Class. redirect_url is a session-bearing URL. A buyer's landing page may show the consumer's prefilled application. Following docs/PII-CLASSES.md it is restricted: never logged, never in an event, never in a postback macro.

    • It is carried only on the ack back to the sender of the lead, which already holds the consumer record.
    • The emission matrix gains an ack column. restricted is allowed only in disposition.redirect_url, and only to the sender of the acknowledged lead/post. Every other row matches event.

3. Money-bearing lifecycle events

When the receiver of a lead reports that lead's commercial fate to its sender, details SHOULD use these shapes. Examples are platform to publisher, or buyer to platform.

Event Meaning details
ACCEPTED Sold. Also emitted for a sale already reported synchronously, so a postback subscriber that is not the posting server learns of it. payout_cents (> 0), currency, revision, sold_at
REJECTED Unsold after pending. Also emitted for an unsold result already reported synchronously, so a postback subscriber learns of it. revision, optional rejection_reason
REFUNDED Returned or reversed after a sale. payout_cents (< 0), currency, revision, returned_at, optional return_reason

Field rules:

  • payout_cents is a signed delta, within -100000000–100000000 and never 0. It is the change this event makes to what the receiver owes the sender for the lead. The sum over all events for one lead is the net amount reported. Float major units are never valid.
  • One currency per lead. Every money event for a lead carries the same currency. A receiver whose owed amount for a lead spans two currencies, or has none, MUST NOT report it under either label: it sends no money event and records the refusal for its operator.
  • A sale that owes nothing is not reported. When a buyer took the lead but the sender is owed 0 (a zero-price payable), there is no money event, because payout_cents is never 0, and no REJECTED, because the lead was not unsold. A sender waiting on pending applies the final_by rule, which matches its payout.
  • revision is an integer ≥ 1, strictly increasing per lead_id for one sender–receiver pair. A sender keeps the highest revision it has seen and ignores a lower or equal one. Retries and out-of-order delivery are then harmless.
  • return_reason is an open enum. Known values are invalid_contact, duplicate, compliance_fail, fraud, consumer_dispute and buyer_error.
  • All fields are internal. The event class rule still holds: no consumer data in details.

Settlement neutrality. These shapes report. They do not invoice. post.pricing remains the platform–buyer billing record, and the deployment's statement of account remains the publisher payable record.

4. URL-template postbacks

subscription gains one optional property:

Field Type Rule
delivery string, enum json \ url_template Default json, which is today's LEP-0001 behaviour, unchanged.

When delivery = url_template, callback_url is a template and these rules apply. A registration that breaks one is refused with LCP-100 and field = callback_url, and no new code is added.

  1. Literal destination.

    • The template is at most 2048 bytes (UTF-8) before expansion, the callback_url limit.
    • The scheme, host, port and path are literal. Macros appear only in the query component, after ?.
    • The existing SSRF rule (no embedded credentials, valid port, public resolved addresses only) runs on the literal host at registration and again before every delivery.
    • Redirects returned by the tracker are not followed.

  2. Closed macro set. Any other {…} token is refused.

    Macro Value
    {lead_id} Event lead_id.
    {external_id} The sender's lead.external_id, or empty.
    {click_id}, {sub1}…{sub5} From lcp.affiliate.tracking on the lead (§5), or empty.
    {event} The event name exactly as sent, for example ACCEPTED.
    {payout} details.payout_cents as a decimal in major units. Uses the ISO 4217 exponent of currency, . as separator, no grouping, and a leading - when negative: 4450 USD → 44.50; -4450 USD → -44.50; 4450 JPY → 4450. Empty when absent.
    {payout_cents} details.payout_cents as an integer, or empty.
    {currency} details.currency, or empty.
    {revision} details.revision, or empty.
    {test} 1 when the event is test traffic, else 0.
    {timestamp} Event timestamp.

  1. Expansion.

    • Every value is percent-encoded (RFC 3986; unreserved characters kept).
    • The expanded URL MUST NOT exceed 4096 bytes. A longer one is a delivery failure, not a truncation.

  2. Request.

    • GET with no body. Any 2xx is success.
    • Retries follow the subscription's existing delivery schedule.
    • The event_types allowlist applies unchanged. A subscriber that needs different tracker URLs per event (a conversion URL for ACCEPTED, an adjustment URL for REFUNDED) registers one subscription per URL.
  3. Signing. Deliveries carry the same headers as a JSON delivery: X-LCP-Timestamp, X-LCP-Event-Id, X-LCP-Idempotency-Key (the event id) and X-LCP-Signature. The signature is computed as in spec/01-transport.md, with the expanded URL in place of the raw request body: <timestamp>\n<idempotency-key>\n<expanded-url>. A tracker that cannot verify ignores the headers.

  4. No consumer data, by construction.

    • No macro resolves to a contact, restricted or prohibited value.
    • Adding a macro requires an LEP, and the new macro's source must be public or internal.
    • Trackers often embed their own API token in the template, so receivers MUST NOT log the expanded query string. They log host, status, latency and delivery id.
  5. Tenancy and addressing. A postback fires only for leads the subscriber sent, the same tenant rule LEP-0001 applies to JSON deliveries. It fires only for events the receiver addresses to the subscriber about its own lead's sale result: a buyer's own ACCEPTED or REJECTED event about a post, which the publisher never sees, does not expand a publisher's template.

5. Registered extension lcp.affiliate.tracking

This carries the sender's own click and sub-ID values on a lead or post so they can be echoed back through postback macros.

{ "lcp.affiliate.tracking": { "click_id": "cl_8f3a2b", "sub1": "fb", "sub2": "adset_44", "sub3": "", "sub4": "", "sub5": "" } }
  • Keys are click_id and sub1–sub5, all optional. Any other key is refused.

  • Values are strings matching ^[A-Za-z0-9._~:-]{0,255}$. The pattern excludes @, +, whitespace and /, so an email address or an E.164 number cannot appear.

    • The LEP-0006 B.3.4 extension rule still applies: a run of 7–15 digits reads as a phone number and is refused with LCP-100. A sender with numeric click ids prefixes them (c_1234567890) or sends them in click_id with a letter.
    • The no-consumer-data rule is normative on the sender. A receiver MAY refuse a value that its detector classes as contact data, with LCP-100.
  • Class internal. Not ping_safe. Never forwarded to a downstream buyer: these values belong to the sender–receiver pair that carried them.

6. Test outcomes

This builds on the existing test envelope field, the X-LCP-Test header and LCP-013. A receiver that advertises features.test_outcomes:

  • MUST answer a test lead or post that carries lcp.test.outcome with that outcome, and MUST NOT route it to a live counterparty, bill it, or count it in any payable or rate. A test lead without the extension follows the receiver's existing test policy (LCP-013), and is answered pending when routed asynchronously.
  • When it relays downstream, it MUST use the downstream's own test mode or not relay at all.

  • MUST honour the registered extension lcp.test.outcome:

    { "lcp.test.outcome": { "outcome": "sold", "payout_cents": 4450 } }
    • outcome is required and is one of the §1 values.
    • payout_cents is optional. When it is absent, the synthetic amount is fixed: payout_cents = 1000 with currency = USD, on the disposition and on the ACCEPTED event alike. A forced price_rejected answers counter_price_cents = 1000, USD. An integrator's test therefore asserts the same values against every receiver.
    • A forced unsold or price_rejected still produces its final event: REJECTED with revision 1 and rejection_reason no_buyer or price_too_high.
    • The extension is legal only when message.test is true. On live traffic it is refused with LCP-013.
    • It takes effect only on a 1.3.0 or later message, because the forced result is reported in disposition (§1's version gate).
  • On a forced pending, MUST send the final test event within 60 seconds, as ACCEPTED with revision 1, so the async path can be exercised.
  • MUST emit the test's §3 events with message.test = true. JSON deliveries carry X-LCP-Test: true; URL-template deliveries expand {test} to 1.
  • SHOULD return, for a forced sold, a redirect_url on its own domain that serves a static confirmation page and records the hit. The integrator can then prove the browser redirect works before go-live.

Invalid A — sold without a payout

{"outcome": "sold", "currency": "USD"} is refused by schema: payout_cents is required when outcome = sold.

Invalid B — macro in the host

"callback_url": "https://{sub1}.track.example.com/pb?e={event}" is refused with LCP-100, field = callback_url, because macros are legal only in the query component.

Invalid C — unknown macro

"callback_url": "https://track.example.com/pb?e={event}&m={email}" is refused with LCP-100: {email} is not in the closed macro set.

Invalid D — forced outcome on live traffic

A lead with message.test = false and extensions["lcp.test.outcome"] is refused with LCP-013 TEST_TRAFFIC_POLICY.

Invalid E — disposition to a 1.2 sender

A receiver acknowledging a message whose lcp.version is 1.2.0 with a disposition violates §1's version gate. Conformance vector SALE-0xx asserts that the 1.2-shaped ack omits it.

Invalid F — contact data in a sub-ID

"sub1": "jane@example.com" fails the lcp.affiliate.tracking pattern and is refused with LCP-100, field = extensions.lcp.affiliate.tracking.sub1.

Privacy, security, and universal-core audit

New consumer-data surfaces. There is one: disposition.redirect_url, classed restricted and carried only back to the sender of the lead, which already holds the record. Every other new field is public or internal. Postback macros resolve only from internal sources, so a tracker never receives consumer data.

Egress (§4).

  • URL-template delivery is a new outbound GET surface. It reuses LEP-0001's SSRF rule, adds a literal-host constraint and no-redirect-following, and bounds URL length.
  • Trackers' own tokens in templates are protected by the no-query-logging rule.
  • Residual risk: a subscriber can point a template at any public host. That is the same as JSON delivery today, and is bounded by the per-tenant subscription limits.

Redirect integrity (§2).

  • Forbidding server-side fetches protects the downstream receiver's measurement and stops a sender from inflating redirect rate.
  • Relay tokens are opaque and expire.
  • Open-redirect risk: a relay only redirects to destinations it received from a downstream ack, never to a URL taken from a request.

Money integrity.

  • All amounts are integer minor units with the existing typo guard.
  • Signed deltas with revisions make returns reconcilable without mutable state on the sender.
  • Nothing here changes a payable.

Test isolation (§6). Forced outcomes are refused on live traffic. Test leads never reach a live counterparty or a payable.

Universal-core audit (SPEC §12).

  • No vertical or market field enters the core. disposition and delivery are vertical-neutral.
  • The affiliate sub-ID convention is market practice, so it is a registered extension, not a core field.
  • Redirect handling applies to any vertical with a consumer handoff.
  • Ping is untouched.

Compatibility, versioning, and deprecation impact

  • MINOR.

    • Optional property on a closed payload (ack.disposition), gated on the acknowledged message's version.
    • Optional property with a default that preserves today's behaviour (subscription.delivery).
    • Documented shapes inside an open object (event.details).
    • Registered extensions.

  • No change for 1.0–1.2 peers.

    • A 1.2 sender never receives disposition.
    • A JSON subscription is unchanged.
    • Events without the new details keys stay valid.
  • rejection_reason description widening is text-only. The field stays an open enum.
  • Deprecation: none.

SDK, OpenAPI, MCP, conformance, and operational impact

Schemas (edited first at implementation):

  • schemas/ack.json: disposition, with if/then for the per-outcome required fields, and legal only on an ack whose status is RECEIVED, ACCEPTED or DUPLICATE.
  • schemas/subscription.json: delivery.
  • governance/EXTENSION-REGISTRY.md: two rows.
  • docs/PII-CLASSES.md: the ack column and the disposition.redirect_url mapping row.
  • Bundles and SHA-256 manifests regenerate.

Ten SDKs: the generated models and schema bundles carry disposition and delivery at v1.3.0. Hand-written helpers (is_sold, format_payout, a URL-template validator, a test_outcome builder) are deferred: check_sdk_surface requires the same surface in all ten SDKs, and the helpers ship together in one later SDK release.

OpenAPI and MCP:

  • the ack response schema gains disposition;
  • the subscription create schema gains delivery;
  • MCP lead-submit tools return disposition unchanged.

Conformance: new suite test-vectors/sale-lifecycle.json, ids SALE-001…:

  • per-outcome ack shapes;
  • the version gate;
  • DUPLICATE repeating the disposition;
  • signed-delta revision ordering;
  • macro expansion, including the JPY exponent and negative payouts;
  • the 4096-byte limit on the expanded URL;
  • a golden HMAC signature for a URL-template GET;
  • every Invalid example above.

Operations:

  • GET /v1/lcp/metrics gains counters for disposition outcome and for postback delivery success and failure by host;
  • per-sender redirect-rate measurement is a SHOULD for relays;
  • reference-platform flags LCP_FEATURE_SALE_DISPOSITION, LCP_FEATURE_URL_POSTBACKS and LCP_FEATURE_TEST_OUTCOMES, each off by default.

Alternatives considered

  • Decimal-string or float prices, the buyer API's own shape. Rejected. Floats round, and a decimal string in one field next to minor units everywhere else (LEP-0010) is worse than either alone. Human-readable decimals live in {payout} and SDK formatters instead.
  • A new disposition message type sent after the ack. Rejected. It doubles round trips for the common synchronous case, and the two-adopter rule applies to new message types. The async case is already covered by events.
  • New event names (SOLD, UNSOLD, RETURNED). Rejected for v1.3.0. ACCEPTED, REJECTED and REFUNDED already carry those meanings, and subscription.event_types is a closed enum. Friendlier names for trackers are a deferred question.
  • Extension-only (lcp.sale.disposition in an ack extension). Rejected. ack has no extensions container, and adding one is a larger core change than one optional object.
  • Keep it deployment-specific, as SpearPointX's planned response body would. Rejected, because every buyer, network and publisher re-invents it, and that is the fork this record exists to prevent.

Rollout, migration, observability, and rollback

  1. Schemas, vectors and the reference platform land behind three feature flags, off by default. capabilities().features advertises each flag only when it is on.
  2. The reference deployment (SpearPointX) enables sale_disposition and test_outcomes for publishers on 1.3.0, then url_postbacks after the security/privacy review signs off.
  3. Publishers move by sending lcp.version 1.3.0. A sender that stays on 1.2.0 keeps today's ack.
  4. Observability: outcome counters, postback success rate by host, redirect rate per sender, and test-versus-live split.
  5. Rollback: turning a flag off removes the advertisement and the behaviour. Acks to 1.3.0 senders omit disposition, which senders MUST tolerate because it is optional. URL-template subscriptions are disabled, not deleted, and resume when the flag returns.

Deferred questions

  • Re-post at the counter price. price_rejected returns counter_price_cents, but a direct lead has no floor field to re-post with. Trigger: a second buyer API with price-reject semantics, or a sender asking to automate re-posts. Candidate: a lead-side floor reusing LEP-0009's bounds.
  • Friendly event aliases for trackers ({event_alias} → sold, unsold, returned). Trigger: tracker-integration feedback that raw names are a support burden.
  • Redirect completion reported downstream. A relay knows when the consumer arrived, and a buyer may want that as an event. Trigger: a buyer asking for it, or a dispute over redirect rate.
  • Shared-lead dispositions (LEP-0003, multiple buyers per lead). Trigger: the first shared-lead deployment that pays publishers per slot.
  • Postback signature for GET. §4.5 substitutes the expanded URL for the body. Review should confirm that this is sufficient against replay through a tracker, given the idempotency key is a header, not part of the URL.
