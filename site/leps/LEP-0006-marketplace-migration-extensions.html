Separation of the two tracks

LEP.md (Decision rules): "Separate capabilities with materially different semantics may share an umbrella discussion, but each must have independently reviewable and independently decidable acceptance criteria." Track A (price feedback on the existing open event surface) and Track B (a new extensions surface on lead/post plus one registered payload shape) share only the migration story; they touch different messages, different risk classes, and different reviewer roles. Each track is accepted, rejected, or deferred independently (the LEP-0001 batch/subscription precedent); the registry row and the decision record name per-track outcomes. This record recommends Track A over the zero-schema-cost event path and Track B over an LEP-carried payload change — the two routes differ precisely because their data lands on different message classes, and the asymmetry is defended below.

Problem statement and evidence

Track A — final-pricing feedback

docs/V1.1-ROADMAP.md Phase 6, "External sale feedback (final_pricing equivalent)": publishers selling the same lead across multiple networks need to report what a ping actually sold for — including external sales — so buyers can refine bids. Design constraint from the same section: publisher→platform feedback of winning price and duration for a ping, keyed by ping ID; "extension or event-detail candidate ({org}.marketplace.final_pricing), not core; must not conflict with the platform's own auction price in post.pricing."

Internal evidence. The protocol already structures one post-sale value fact: the CONVERTED event's details.conversion_value_cents (SPEC.md §4), explicitly called out in §3 as the structured exception because "close value on conversion is a lifecycle fact, not a calculated metric." Nothing analogous exists for the other sale outcomes that matter to a pinged buyer: the lead sold elsewhere, or sold for more than the auction cleared. Buyers bid blind to those outcomes today; the reference network's final_pricing reports exist precisely to close that feedback loop, and the roadmap classifies the mechanism (not the implementation) as a genuine gap.

Wire-surface reality (measured 2026-11-03). The extension candidate name from the roadmap implies a payload extensions surface, but payload.extensions is defined only on schemas/offer.json:248-252 (buyer-side matching criteria, additionalProperties: true). Every delivery message is a strict closed object: schemas/ping.json:206, schemas/lead.json:166, schemas/post.json:176, schemas/bid.json (allowlist), schemas/ack.json — verified by reading each root's additionalProperties: false. A publisher has exactly one message class it sends to the platform post-auction whose content is open by design: event, whose details is additionalProperties: true (schemas/event.json:25-28) and whose event value is an open enumeration with no schema enum (schemas/event.json:16-19; SPEC.md §6 "Open vs. closed enumerations": unknown event values MUST be stored and passed through; adding values is a MINOR). Track A therefore rides the event surface — the zero-schema-cost route, exactly the mechanism LEP-0003 §4 used for CONTACTED reporting — and this record documents why the alternative (a payload change) was rejected there.

Track B — the {org}.form.questions home

Roadmap Phase 6, "Freeform questions extension shape": partners migrating from legacy ping/post networks carry questions[] (question_text/answer_text) data with no standard home in LCP; the design constraint is the registered extension pattern {org}.form.questions — post-only, never ping_safe — so the controlled-taxonomy decision in home_services stays intact.

Internal evidence — the destination is already promised but does not exist. verticals/home_services.json:5: "Publishers normalize brand-specific form questions into this controlled taxonomy; unsupported source questions remain in an approved post-only extension or source archive." That approved extension surface is not in the protocol today: there is no extensions field on lead or post (closes above). The one "post-only" free-text escape the vertical registry ships — free_text (verticals/home_services.json:103, ping_safe: false) — is a single string with no question/answer structure, which is exactly why the roadmap asks for a registered shape rather than leaning on it.

Internal evidence — the reference platform already consumes a lead-side extensions object that its own schema forbids. The claim-language restriction evaluates namespaced evidence from the lead payload: matching.py:193-196 reads payload.get("extensions", {}).get("lcp.platform.claim_language") and fail-closes (claim_language_evidence_missing) when absent; matching.py:116-120 similarly reads extensions.get("lcp.platform.required_consent_purposes") / "lcp.platform.contact_purpose" from the offer side (which is legal — schemas/offer.json:248). A lead carrying the evidence block the matcher demands fails the published lead schema — measured: Additional properties are not allowed ('extensions' was unexpected) at (root) (run recorded below). SPEC.md §6 line 655 and §3 lines 224-235 both describe the extensions object as the mechanism for deployment-specific data; §6:661-662 even lists "vertical attributes extensions" among open objects. The protocol prose and the reference matcher anticipate an extensions surface on the intake/delivery messages that the schemas do not ship. Track B closes that gap with the smallest expressible change, and the registered form.questions shape gives migrating partners the structured home the vertical description already promises.

External evidence. The roadmap comparison (2026-08-18) observed both mechanisms live in a major US home-services network's ping/post API: final_pricing sale reports and per-campaign questions[] arrays carried alongside delivery. Both are adoption-visible capabilities, not theoretical.

  • Track B — core protocol (additive MINOR, schema change): one optional extensions object on the lead and post payloads (schemas/lead.json, schemas/post.json), keys constrained to the registry namespace format; plus the registered payload shape {org}.form.questions (array of {question_id?, question_text, answer_text} with bounds and scrub rules) — a registered extension per SPEC.md §6/§3 and governance/EXTENSION-REGISTRY.md, not a schema field. ping, bid, ack, call, event, and every vertical are unchanged.
  • Reference implementation: publisher-side FINAL_PRICING acceptance + advertisement (Track A); lead/post extensions passthrough and delivery, form.questions validation at support-advertisement boundaries, erasure coverage (Track B). Implementation-phase obligations, gated on per-track acceptance.
  • Registry rows: lcp.marketplace.final_pricing and lcp.form.questions proposed in governance/EXTENSION-REGISTRY.md with this record (status: proposed-on-acceptance).
  • SDK / OpenAPI / MCP / vectors: change-gated at implementation (roadmap §Change gates); listed in the impact section. This Draft authorizes none of it.
  • Not in scope: any change to post.pricing or payable/dispute semantics; multi-currency price semantics (deferred roadmap item — see Deferred questions); questions in vertical attributes or in pings (explicitly forbidden by design); an extensions surface on call payloads (deferred with trigger); a schema mechanism for validating namespaced payload shapes (convention + receiver rules today, like LEP-0003's report rules); DLP vendor integration (the platform MAY scrub with whatever deterministic tooling a deployment runs; no normative algorithm is invented here).

Proposed wire shape

Track A — FINAL_PRICING event (publisher → platform)

A new documented known value of the open event enumeration. No schema file changes; event.json already stores and passes through any string (schemas/event.json:16-19 $comment: "No enum constraint — this is an open enumeration per SPEC.md §6").

Recommended details shape (the same SHOULD-style guidance pattern as CONVERTED/DISPUTED/CONTACTED, SPEC.md §4):

Field Type Notes
ping_id string The ping this report describes, as the platform issued it. Optional: a publisher that lost the auction may still know the winning external price.
sale_scope string Open enum, known values external (sold outside this platform's auction) and internal_auction (sold through it). Unknown values stored and passed through (§6).
winning_price_cents integer Winning price in integer minor units of currency. MUST be > 0 and MUST NOT exceed 100000000 (typo guard: 1,000,000 major units); receivers MUST treat larger values as malformed reports.
currency string ISO 4217 alpha-3, uppercase (core currency discipline, schemas/core.json $defs/currency).
duration_seconds integer Contact duration of the winning sale when the sale is call-based; 0 for form sales. MUST be >= 0 and <= 86400.
sold_at string (date-time) When the winning sale occurred.

Deployment-specific additions ride namespaced keys inside details under the registered {org}.marketplace.final_pricing namespace (the roadmap's own candidate name, kept meaningful on this route — see Registry notes). The event payload's existing external_reference (schemas/event.json:29-31) MAY carry the external network's sale record id.

Normative semantics:

  • Settlement neutrality (the roadmap's conflict clause, made operational). post.pricing (SPEC.md §4) remains the sole billing and dispute record between platform and buyer. A FINAL_PRICING report MUST NOT change any payable, refund a charged price, adjust post.pricing fields, or re-price an in-flight auction. Receivers consume it for analytics and bid refinement only (the roadmap's stated purpose: "so buyers can refine bids"). A report contradicting the platform's own auction outcome is not an error — it is the point (external sales); disputes about it ride the existing ack/rejection and dispute paths, not a new one.
  • Trust model: authenticated-sender attestation, format+bounds sanity only — the LEP-0003 CONTACTED model. The platform cannot verify what a lead sold for on another network; FINAL_PRICING values are attestations by the authenticated publisher (event:submit scope + HMAC signature, SPEC.md §9), scoped to one lead_id. Malformed or out-of-bound reports are validation rejections, not stores.
  • Keying and replay. The event's required lead_id keys the report; details.ping_id keys the correction chain. Replay collapses on the event message.id (the reference platform's existing mechanism: same-id-same-bytes → DUPLICATE, reuse-with-different-bytes → LCP-005 409; router.py:1260-1283). Corrections are normal: a later report for the same (lead_id, ping_id) supersedes the earlier one when its payload timestamp is newer (ties: higher message id lexicographic). Receivers SHOULD keep report history and MUST expose the superseding view.
  • PII discipline. event is a non-PII message class (SPEC.md §4 table: the event row reads None, spec/02-envelope.md:48; the "(hashes only)" qualifier belongs to the ping row, spec/02-envelope.md:44 — quote misattribution fixed post-dossier, #185 F3). A FINAL_PRICING report carries no consumer data at all: the contact is identified by lead_id alone; phone/email/hash values MUST NOT appear in details. Receivers MUST reject PII-bearing reports at ingest (see invalid example A-1; honest status recorded there — the open details cannot express this in schema, so it is an ingest-behavior requirement).
  • Capability gating. A publisher MUST send FINAL_PRICING only to a platform whose §8 advertisement lists it in capabilities().events and whose lcp_versions contains a parsed major == 1, minor >= 1 full-semver element (the normative membership rule of SPEC.md §8, landed via LEP-0003 F6/T10). The reference platform today: router.py:1730-1763 advertises thirteen values and ["1.0.0", "1.1.0"] (router.py:1738 reading validation.py:23, router.py:1749-1763). Zero schema cost, nonzero platform cost (measured path): the publisher-reportable set _PUBLISHER_EVENT_TYPES (router.py:107-112: REJECTED, DISPUTED, CONSENT_WITHDRAWN, ERASURE_REQUEST) does not contain FINAL_PRICING, and the sender authorization at router.py:1250-1258 rejects any other name with LCP-002 ("Sender is not authorized for this lead event"). Acceptance therefore obligates the implementation PR to add FINAL_PRICING to _PUBLISHER_EVENT_TYPES behind the v1.1 gate and advertise it — the exact F5/T6 correction pattern LEP-0003 recorded. FINAL_PRICING MUST NOT be added to _EVENT_STATUS_TARGETS (router.py:74-82): it asserts no lifecycle transition; a price report does not move status (see §3 status transition table, spec/03-payloads.md:184).

Track B — extensions on lead/post, and the registered {org}.form.questions shape

B.1 The payload surface (LEP-carried schema change)

Add one optional field to lead and post, identical shape, drafted as the overlay validated below:

"extensions": {
  "type": "object",
  "description": "Namespaced LCP extensions (SPEC.md section 6;
    governance/EXTENSION-REGISTRY.md). Keys must follow
    {org}.{division}.{purpose}; lcp.core.* is reserved. Unknown
    namespaces are ignored by implementations that do not recognize
    them.",
  "propertyNames": {
    "pattern": "^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_-]*(\\.[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_-]*){2,}$",
    "not": { "pattern": "^lcp\\.core\\." }
  },
  "additionalProperties": true
}
  • Where, and why exactly there. lead (publisher→platform intake) and post (platform→buyer delivery): the data must ride the delivery itself — the buyer needs the questions when it works the lead, not in a side-channel notification (the event alternative is analyzed and rejected under Alternatives). call is deliberately excluded at v1.1 (telephony Q&A migration has no adopter evidence yet; deferred with trigger). ping/bid/ack stay closed — that is the "never ping_safe" guardrail made structural (see B.3).
  • Key shape is expressible and enforced: the propertyNames pattern requires ≥3 lowercase segments per the registry format and forbids lcp.core.* claims per the registry's rejection grounds (governance/EXTENSION-REGISTRY.md, "Rejection grounds"). Measured: "questions" and "lcp.core.form_questions" REJ under the overlay; three-segment namespaces PASS.
  • Values stay deployment-defined (additionalProperties: true per namespace): the core cannot and does not validate namespaced payloads — same as schemas/offer.json:248-252 and event.details. Shape authority for a registered namespace is this LEP's §B.2 for {org}.form.questions; receiver-side rules below stand in for the schema, and this record states that boundary honestly rather than implying a validator that does not exist.
  • Capability gating (mandatory, measured): lead/post are closed objects, so the §6 "unknown optional fields ignored" guarantee does not cover this field (spec/04-errors.md:12-18 — closed-object additions "rely on version negotiation"). A v1.1 extensions-bearing lead sent to a receiver advertising only ["1.0.0"] fails that receiver's strict allowlist — measured: Additional properties are not allowed ('extensions' was unexpected). Senders gate emission on the receiver's parsed lcp_versions ≥ 1.1 (§8, LEP-0003 F6 rule); absent extensions = bit-for-bit v1.0 behavior.
  • The matcher gap closes. With this surface legal, the claim-language evidence matching.py:193-196 already reads becomes sendable; today that code path is only reachable if validation is bypassed (it is not — such leads are rejected upstream — so the predicate currently fail-closes for schema-validating senders, which is the trap this fix removes). No matcher code change is required by Track B beyond passing the object through validation.

B.2 The registered payload shape {org}.form.questions

Normative for any org registering the namespace (reference platform registers lcp.form.questions; other adopters register {org}.form.questions for their own org id):

"extensions": {
  "acme.form.questions": [
    {
      "question_id": "own_or_rent",
      "question_text": "Do you own the property or are you renting?",
      "answer_text": "Own outright"
    }
  ]
}
  • Array of at most 25 entries.
  • Each entry an object: question_id optional, ^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_-]{0,63}$; question_text required string, ≤ 300 characters; answer_text required string, ≤ 1000 characters; no other keys (strings only — nested objects are out of the registered shape, which keeps content scanning tractable).
  • Total serialized extensions object — the bound hierarchy, with the arithmetic (computed post-dossier, #185 F1 / #197; measured plant in the Revision section): the shape bounds above admit a worst-case shape-legal form.questions block — 25 entries, question_id at the 64-character maximum, question_text 300, answer_text 1000 — serializing to 35,640 bytes ASCII (25 × (1300 + 64) + 1,540 structural) and 133,140 bytes worst-case UTF-8 (maxLength counts codepoints; 4-byte text: 25 × (4 × 1300 + 64) + 1,540). Both exceed 32 KiB. Receivers SHOULD reject extensions totals beyond 32 KiB (32,768 B) — an auditable, cheap-to-scan abuse target that is explicitly a soft policy bound, not a schema property: a fully shape-conformant worst-case sender exceeds it, so a receiver that hard-rejects at 32 KiB rejects shape-legal messages. Reconciling the policy target with the shape ceiling (lower the per-field bounds until the worst case fits, or move the total bound) is the Review re-tune decision tracked in #197; this revision states the arithmetic without renumbering the proposed bounds (the B.1/B.2 fences carry committed plant digests and Track B is unimplemented — the numbers are a Review item, not a silent record-side choice). A deployment MAY set tighter limits and MUST NOT loosen the shape bounds above. Values under unrecognized namespaces are schema-unbounded (B.1's additionalProperties: true; measured: a 1,000,000-byte value under acme.a.b PASSES the overlay). The operative hard ceiling on the wire is the transport body cap: the reference platform rejects request bodies above max_body_bytes — default 2,000,000 bytes (config.py:37, enforced pre-parse at service.py:103-105 as LCP-001 413; live 413 measured in the Revision battery) — capping whole messages of every class, extensions included.
  • A receiver that has implemented support for a registered form.questions namespace MUST validate entries against this shape and MUST either accept the message or reject it (LCP-100) with the offending namespace identified — it MUST NOT silently store a non-conforming array. Receivers that do not recognize the namespace ignore its value (extension forward-compatibility, governance/EXTENSION-REGISTRY.md "Extension payload location"). These are receiver rules, not schema rules (B.1's boundary statement).

B.3 The guardrails the roadmap set — "post-only, never ping_safe"

  1. Structural: the field exists only on lead and post. A ping carrying extensions is rejected by the unchanged strict allowlist — measured REJ under both the published and overlay trees (invalid example B-3). The auction PII firewall (SPEC.md §4 ping allowlist, LCP-008) gains nothing to leak.
  2. Normative projection ban: a platform MUST NOT derive ping attributes, compliance_flags, or any other ping field from extension content. Matching against vertical taxonomy uses attributes only; extensions are content, not routing fuel.
  3. Taxonomy integrity: publishers MUST NOT encode question text into vertical attributes fields to smuggle it into pings — the existing ping_safe tagging and reserved-core-name rules (tools/check_reserved_fields.py, SPEC.md §3/§12) already police that boundary; this LEP adds no exemption. The controlled-taxonomy normalization step of home_services.json:5 is unchanged: questions that fit the taxonomy are also normalized there; the extension carries the verbatim residue, which never pings.

  4. PII discipline inside the extension (the hard review question, answered head-on). Freeform question/answer text can smuggle consumer data, and post is already a full-PII message — so the real risk axes are:

    • Duplication defeats lifecycle keys. Suppression and erasure events (CONSENT_WITHDRAWN, ERASURE_REQUEST, SPEC.md §4) and the §9 dedup machinery operate on phone_hash/email_hash — a phone number inside answer_text is invisible to those flows (unhashed, unsearchable by hash, retained past field-scoped deletion in deployments that index structured fields). MUST (publisher): question/answer text MUST NOT contain phone numbers, email addresses, street addresses, government identifiers, payment credentials, consent tokens or evidence values (those belong in consumer, compliance.consent_evidence[], or attachments[]), and MUST NOT duplicate the consumer's name when the structured consumer block already carries it.
    • Retention scope. An extensions value is stored lead content: it inherits §9 PII retention and MUST be purged by the same erasure execution as the rest of the record (platform erasure paths delete the stored lead/post record wholesale; this LEP states explicitly that extensions is in that record's PII scope — free text must never live in a side store excluded from erasure).
    • Egress/abuse volume. B.2 states the hierarchy with the arithmetic (post-dossier, #185 F1): shape bounds cap the worst-case shape-legal registered block at 133,140 bytes; the 32 KiB SHOULD is a scan-cheap soft policy target above that, not a schema ceiling; unrecognized-namespace values are schema-unbounded and land on the transport body cap (default 2 MB, config.py:37 / service.py:103-105) as the operative hard bound — #197 tracks the bounds re-tune at Review.
    • Platform MAY scrub/truncate, deterministically. A platform MAY apply a deterministic redaction pass (pattern-based contact-identifier redaction) or truncate over-bound values before delivering a post; it MUST apply the same policy to every message (no per-recipient discretion), MUST log each redaction/truncation event against the lead_id, and SHOULD reject (rather than deliver) content matching credential/token shapes listed above. A deployment MAY configure reject-vs-redact policy; neither mode may silently drop the whole extensions object on a supporting receiver. The reviewer question this record pre-empts for the dossier: should redaction be a MUST on the platform side (the publisher-side scrub is a MUST already)? The Draft keeps platform redaction at MAY because the protocol cannot guarantee every platform runs DLP before it can refuse the lead without breaking delivery in markets where the publisher is compliant; Review should test that reasoning.
    • No new recipient class: the data crosses exactly the same publisher→platform→winning-buyer PII path the lead already uses (SPEC.md §4 post is the full-PII channel; §9 transport/auth requirements unchanged). This is data-shape guidance inside an existing boundary, not a boundary change — but it does move a PII-adjacent surface (freeform consumer answers now ride a protocol-defined extension), so LEP.md requires the named human security/privacy reviewer or the ARB-Security co-sign at decision.
  5. Consent/scrub evidence MUST NOT ride form.questions. Answer text such as "her consent code is 4471" is a compliance-evidence value with a structured home (consent_evidence[]); free-text evidence is unverifiable and unrevocable. Invalid example B-4 covers a contact-identifier case; the same receiver rule covers tokens.

Privacy, security, and universal-core audit

Track A — privacy. No consumer data on the wire: event is a non-PII message class (SPEC.md §4 table), the report is keyed by lead_id + details.ping_id only, and PII-shaped details values are ingest rejections (A-1; the receiver rule that the open schema cannot express). The report references only parties who already know the ping happened (the publisher and the platform). Commercial-sensitivity note: a winning price from a third-party network is the publisher's data to share and the platform's to hold — retention follows the §9 PII policy already applied to lifecycle events; a deployment MAY scope reports to the analytics store's own retention.

Track A — security/abuse. Forged or poisoned price reports are the abuse axis: blast radius is bid-refinement analytics, never settlement (the settlement-neutrality MUSTs), under the authenticated-sender attestation model (event:submit scope + HMAC per SPEC.md §9) — the same "unverifiable-by-design" honesty LEP-0003 recorded for contact reports. Bounds (integer minor units, ≤ 10^8, duration_seconds ≤ 86400, required ISO-4217) refuse typo/fuzz payloads; correction chains are keyed, monotone-expire, and auditable (router.py:1260-1283 event dedup unchanged). Replay: same-id collapse + LCP-005 id-reuse rejection, inherited mechanisms. Rate limits apply per §9.

  • LEP-carried payload change (a final_pricing field on an existing message or a new report message). The payload extensions surface this Draft adds for Track B would also have worked post-Track-B — but Track A's independence (it needs nothing Track B adds) keeps the tracks decidable in either order, which is the point of the separation rule.
  • Namespaced event value instead of a documented known value (acme.marketplace.final_pricing as the event string itself). Rejected: a universal lifecycle vocabulary is what makes the signal readable across publisher→platform pairs without org-by-org vocabulary discovery, and §6 explicitly prices that as a MINOR on the open enum; the roadmap's extension name is preserved inside the details (where deployment specifics belong).
  • Putting the price into post.pricing (extending the settlement block). Rejected — direct roadmap conflict-clause violation: the auction price and an attested external price are different facts with different authority; co-locating them would let an unverifiable attestation touch billing.
  • Keeping final-pricing deployment-specific (no LEP). Available until acceptance and legitimate as a private route (registry is open), but rejected as the destination: the mechanism's value is cross-platform bid refinement, which vendor vocabularies cannot standardize; the roadmap lists it for adoption ("the mechanism, not the implementation details").

Track B:

  • Event-channel for questions (zero payload change). Rejected on delivery semantics: questions are part of the delivered lead content; a post must be self-contained. Replaying questions as post-delivery events adds ordering (buyer must join two surfaces before working the lead), completeness (a dropped event is silently missing job context), and a second ingest path for the same data — the "partial delivery" class LCP's post design avoids. Also rejected on precedent: lead/ call carry content blocks directly; events carry notifications.
  • Vertical attributes home (extend home_services etc.). Rejected by design constraint and by the vertical's own text (home_services.json:5): the controlled taxonomy is the matching substrate; unbounded per-brand question keys would break the taxonomy and re-open the §12/§3 reserved-name and ping_safe audit surface with every campaign.
  • Lean on free_text (home_services.json:103) alone. Rejected: structure-free single string loses question/answer pairing and defeats the shape bounds + receiver validation this LEP defines; it remains available as today's informal practice, which the roadmap asks to replace with an "approved post-only extension."
  • extensions on every message type at once (lead/post/call/event/ack). Rejected as scope pressure: ping/bid/ack must stay closed (firewall/response-minimalism); call lacks adopter evidence (Deferred question D1); event.details is already open (no field needed).
  • Keeping questions deployment-specific (status quo + source archive). Rejected with evidence: the source archive helps nobody at delivery time, and the matcher/schema contradiction (Problem section) means a deployment-private extension is illegal on the published schemas today — the status quo is not a neutral option, it is a broken one.

Both tracks: the umbrella was kept to one LEP because LEP.md allows umbrella discussions sharing the migration story while mandating independent decidability; splitting into two numbers was considered and would be acceptable — the maintainers may renumber at Review without restarting the discussion if the ARB prefers that.

Deferred questions

  • D1 — call payload extensions. Telephony intake has question/answer analogues (IVR keys, agent-asked questions; schemas/call.json:148 already says "platform-specific IVR data goes in extensions" while call.json ships no extensions field — the same promised-destination gap as Track B). Trigger: two independent adopters migrating call-center Q&A, or an IVR attestation requirement; then add call to the B.1 surface (a pure copy of the object, no new shape).
  • D2 — Multi-currency price reports. winning_price_cents is an integer in a REQUIRED currency; cross-currency comparison is the roadmap's deferred "Multi-currency auction semantics" item. Trigger: any auction where bids and final-pricing reports can be denominated differently (a platform operating two currencies); until then reports MUST use the auction currency when sale_scope is internal_auction and publishers SHOULD report the currency the external sale settled in (no FX guessing in the platform).
  • D3 — Schema-enforceable validation of registered shapes. Today namespaced values are validated only by receiver conformance to the registry spec (B.2; B-4 is PASS in the schema by design). Trigger: a third registered shape with bounds worth a shared mechanism (a $defs + registry-metadata scheme), or one incident of over-bound delivery causing harm.
  • D4 — Signed external-sale proof. FINAL_PRICING is attestation-grade (trust model); signed third-party proofs (e.g. a network-issued sale receipt) are out of scope. Trigger: adopters paying real money on these signals or dispute flows demanding proof.
  • D5 — Payable/dispute interaction. A report revealing a higher external price could tempt refund claims on lower auction prices. Settlement neutrality (Track A MUSTs) says no today; a market that wants price-guarantee clauses needs a commercial policy, not a wire change. Trigger: two independent adopters' contracts asserting such claims.
  • D6 — Per-namespace capability advertisement. §8 events is a list of event values; there is no "extensions supported / which namespaces policy applies" surface (redaction policy varies by platform). Trigger: interop friction when one platform redacts and another rejects.
  • D7 — DLP MUST vs MAY (B.3.4): DECIDED 2026-11-03 (maintainer). The platform side stays MAY (best-effort), with the publisher MUST governing. Rationale: the shipped implementation already rejects deterministically detectable contact shapes at lead ingest (LCP-100, nothing stored) and redacts them at post emission with a per-lead audit row; non-contact shapes named by this question (street address, government identifier, payment credential, consent token/evidence) have no deterministic detector, so elevating the platform side to a universal MUST would require a DLP dependency and would make delivery availability depend on an unverifiable classifier — the tradeoff this question flagged. Structured homes for those values (core consumer/ location/compliance fields) remain the conformant route. Revisit trigger: an adopter requires platform-enforced non-contact PII detection, or a maintained DLP dependency becomes acceptable to the project — either reopens this as a Review item.
